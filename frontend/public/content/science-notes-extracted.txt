Chapter 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations [3 Marks Marks | Unit I]
1. Key Concepts & Definitions:
• Chemical Reaction: A process in which chemical bonds break and form new substances with entirely
different chemical properties.
• Balanced Chemical Equation: An equation where the total number of atoms of each element is equal
on both reactant and product sides, strictly satisfying the Law of Conservation of Mass.
2. Types of Chemical Reactions:

1. Combination Reaction: Two or more reactants combine to form a single product. Example: 2Mg + O₂
→ 2MgO (Dazzling white flame).
2. Decomposition Reaction: A single reactant breaks down into simpler products via Heat (Thermal),
Light (Photolytic), or Electricity (Electrolytic). Example: 2FeSO₄(s) Δ→ Fe₂O₃(s) + SO₂(g) + SO₃(g).
3. Displacement Reaction: A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its salt
solution. Example: Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) [Blue] → FeSO₄(aq) [Pale Green] + Cu(s).
4. Double Displacement: Exchange of ions between two compounds forming an insoluble precipitate.
Example: Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s) [White ppt] + 2NaCl(aq).
5. Redox Reactions: Simultaneous Oxidation (gain of oxygen / loss of hydrogen) and Reduction (loss of
oxygen / gain of hydrogen). Example: CuO + H₂ Δ→ Cu + H₂O (CuO is reduced to Cu; H₂ is oxidized to
H₂O).
3. Corrosion & Rancidity: Corrosion is the slow oxidation of metals by atmospheric gases/moisture
(e.g., rusting of iron: Fe₂O₃·xH₂O). Rancidity is the oxidation of fats/oils causing unpleasant odor;
prevented by flushing nitrogen gas or adding antioxidants.
Chapter 2: Acids, Bases and Salts [6 Marks Marks | Unit I]
1. Definitions & Indicators:
• Acids: Produce H⁺(aq) / H₃O⁺ ions in aqueous solution; turn blue litmus red; pH < 7.
• Bases: Produce OH⁻(aq) ions in water; turn red litmus blue; feel soapy; pH > 7.
• Indicators: Natural (Litmus, Turmeric) and Synthetic (Phenolphthalein: colorless in acid, pink in base;
Methyl Orange: red in acid, yellow in base). Olfactory indicators change smell (Onion, Vanilla).
2. Chemical Properties & Reactions:
• Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen gas (tested via pop-sound burning test).
• Acid + Metal Carbonate/Bicarbonate → Salt + Water + CO₂ (turns lime water milky due to CaCO₃
precipitate).
• Neutralization Reaction: Acid + Base → Salt + Water (Exothermic process).
3. pH Scale & Importance: pH = -log[H⁺]. Range 0 to 14. Neutral pH = 7. Tooth decay starts below pH
5.5. Acid rain has pH < 5.6.
4. Important Industrial Chemical Compounds:
• Bleaching Powder (CaOCl₂): Produced by action of chlorine on dry slaked lime Ca(OH)₂. Used as
disinfectant and bleaching agent.
• Baking Soda (NaHCO₃): Sodium hydrogen carbonate. Mild non-corrosive base. Used in baking powder
and antacids.
• Washing Soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O): Sodium carbonate decahydrate. Used in glass/soap industry and
removing permanent water hardness.
• Plaster of Paris (CaSO₄·½H₂O): Calcium sulphate hemihydrate. Prepared by heating Gypsum
(CaSO₄·2H₂O) at 373 K. Sets hard upon adding water.
Chapter 3: Metals and Non-metals [5 Marks Marks | Unit I]
1. Physical & Chemical Comparison:
• Metals are malleable, ductile, sonorous, good conductors of heat/electricity (Silver is best, Lead is
poorest). Mercury is liquid metal; Iodine is lustrous non-metal; Gallium/Cesium melt on palm.
• Amphoteric Oxides: Metal oxides that react with both acids and bases to yield salt and water (e.g.,
Al₂O₃ and ZnO).
• Reactivity Series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au. Potassium and
sodium are stored under kerosene oil to prevent violent reaction with air/water.

2. Ionic Bonding: Formed by complete transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal (e.g., NaCl,
MgCl₂). High melting points, soluble in water, conduct electricity in molten or aqueous state.
3. Metallurgy & Extraction:
• Roasting: Heating sulfide ores strongly in the presence of excess air (2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂).
• Calcination: Heating carbonate ores strongly in limited air (ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂).
• Thermite Process: Reduction of iron oxide using aluminum powder (Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ +
Heat), used for welding railway tracks.
Chapter 4: Carbon and its Compounds [7 Marks Marks | Unit I]
1. Covalent Bonding & Tetravalency: Carbon has atomic number 6 (configuration 2,4).
Forms covalent bonds by sharing electrons. Exhibits Catenation (self-linking property)
and Tetravalency.
2. Allotropes of Carbon: Diamond (rigid 3D structure, hardest natural substance, insulator), Graphite
(hexagonal layers, smooth/slippery, good conductor due to free electrons), and Fullerenes (C-60,
buckyball).
3. Hydrocarbons & Homologous Series:
• Alkanes (C_n H_{2n+2}): Saturated single bond (e.g., Methane CH₄, Ethane C₂H₆).
• Alkenes (C_n H_{2n}): Unsaturated double bond (e.g., Ethene C₂H₄).
• Alkynes (C_n H_{2n-2}): Unsaturated triple bond (e.g., Ethyne C₂H₂).
• Homologous Series: Series of organic compounds with same functional group where adjacent
members differ by -CH₂- unit and 14 u atomic mass.
4. Functional Groups: Alcohol (-OH), Aldehyde (-CHO), Ketone (>C=O), Carboxylic Acid (-COOH),
Halogens (-Cl, -Br).
5. Important Organic Reactions:
• Esterification: Ethanoic acid + Ethanol → Ethyl Ethanoate (sweet smelling ester) + Water (catalyst:
conc. H₂SO₄).
• Saponification: Ester + NaOH → Sodium Ethanoate (Soap) + Ethanol.
• Soaps vs. Detergents: Soaps are sodium/potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids (form scum with
hard water). Detergents are ammonium/sulfonate salts (effective in hard water). Micelle formation cleans
grease.
UNIT II: WORLD OF LIVING — BIOLOGY (25 MARKS)
Chapter 5: Life Processes [8 Marks Marks | Unit II]
1. Nutrition: Autotrophic (Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 12H₂O + Sunlight/Chlorophyll →
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O) and Heterotrophic (Amoeba holozoic nutrition via pseudopodia;
Human digestive tract).
• Human Digestive System: Salivary Amylase digests starch → Pepsin in stomach digests protein
(acidic medium HCl) → Bile from liver emulsifies fats & creates alkaline medium → Trypsin & Lipase in
small intestine complete digestion → Villi absorb nutrients.
2. Respiration: Breakdown of Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) → Pyruvate (3-carbon) in cytoplasm.
Fate of Pyruvate: (a) Absence of O₂ (Yeast): Ethanol + CO₂ + 2 ATP; (b) Lack of O₂
(Muscle cramps): Lactic Acid + 2 ATP; (c) Presence of O₂ (Mitochondria): CO₂ + H₂O +
38 ATP.

3. Transportation: Human double circulation (Systemic & Pulmonary). Heart
chambers: 4-chambered (Right atrium/ventricle carry deoxygenated blood; Left
atrium/ventricle carry oxygenated blood). Plant transport: Xylem transports
water/minerals (transpiration pull); Phloem translocates sucrose (requires ATP).
4. Excretion: Human excretory system (Kidneys, Ureters, Urinary Bladder, Urethra).
Functional unit: Nephron (Bowman's Capsule, Glomerulus filtration, Selective

Chapter 6: Control and Coordination [6 Marks Marks | Unit II]
1. Nervous System & Reflex Arc: Neuron structure (Dendrite → Cell Body → Axon →
Synaptic Terminal). Synapse is the chemical gap crossed by neurotransmitters. Reflex
Arc: Receptor → Sensory Neuron → Spinal Cord (Relay Neuron) → Motor Neuron →
Effector Muscle.
2. Human Brain: Forebrain (Cerebrum: main thinking area, memory), Midbrain
(involuntary actions), Hindbrain (Cerebellum: posture/balance; Medulla: blood
pressure, salvation, vomiting; Pons: respiration rate).
3. Plant Hormones (Phytohormones): Auxin (cell elongation, phototropism bending
toward light), Gibberellin (stem growth), Cytokinin (cell division in fruits/seeds),
Abscisic Acid (stress hormone, inhibits growth, wilting of leaves). Plant movements:
Tropic (directional: photo-, geo-, hydro-, chemo-) vs Nastic (non-directional: *Mimosa
pudica* touch response).
4. Human Endocrine Glands & Hormones: Pituitary (Growth Hormone), Thyroid
(Thyroxin - requires iodine to prevent Goitre), Pancreas (Insulin - regulates blood
sugar, prevents Diabetes), Adrenal (Adrenaline - fight or flight hormone), Testes
(Testosterone), Ovaries (Estrogen).
Chapter 7: Reproduction in Organisms [7 Marks Marks | Unit II]
1. DNA Copying & Asexual Reproduction: DNA replication creates cellular machinery;
minor inaccuracies cause species variation. Asexual modes: Binary Fission (*Amoeba*,
*Leishmania*), Multiple Fission (*Plasmodium*), Fragmentation (*Spirogyra*),
Regeneration (*Planaria*, *Hydra*), Budding (*Hydra*, Yeast), Spore Formation
(*Rhizopus*), Vegetative Propagation (*Bryophyllum* leaf notches).
2. Sexual Reproduction in Plants: Flower anatomy (Stamen = Male: Anther + Filament;
Pistil = Female: Stigma + Style + Ovary). Pollination (Self vs Cross). Double
Fertilization: Syngamy (Male gamete + Egg → Zygote → Embryo) & Triple Fusion
(Endosperm). Post-fertilization: Ovule → Seed; Ovary → Fruit.
3. Human Reproductive Systems: Male (Testes in Scrotum for 2-3°C lower temp for
sperms, Testosterone, Vas Deferens, Prostate/Seminal Vesicles). Female (Ovaries,
Fallopian Tube/Oviduct = site of fertilization, Uterus = implantation site, Placenta
nourish fetus). Unfertilized egg leads to Menstruation (2-8 days).
4. Reproductive Health & Contraception: STDs (Bacterial: Gonorrhea, Syphilis; Viral:
Warts, HIV-AIDS). Contraception: Barriers (Condoms - prevent STDs), Chemical (Oral
Pills), IUCDs (Copper-T), Surgical (Vasectomy in males, Tubectomy in females).

2. Mendel's Experiments (*Pisum sativum*):
• Monohybrid Cross (TT × tt): F₁ generation = All Tall (Tt). F₂ generation Phenotypic Ratio = 3 : 1 (3

UNIT III & IV: PHYSICS PHENOMENA (25 MARKS)
Chapter 9: Light – Reflection and Refraction [8 Marks Marks | Unit III]
1. Spherical Mirrors: Concave (Converging, negative f) and Convex (Diverging,
positive f, wide field of view for rear-view mirrors). Focal relation: f = R / 2.
• Mirror Formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u | Magnification: m = -v/u = h'/h. (Negative m = Real/Inverted;
Positive m = Virtual/Erect).
2. Refraction & Refractive Index: Snell's Law: sin(i)/sin(r) = constant = n₂₁. Absolute
Refractive Index n = c / v. Glass slab causes lateral displacement.
3. Spherical Lenses: Convex (Converging) and Concave (Diverging).
• Lens Formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u | Magnification: m = +v/u = h'/h.
• Optical Power (P): P = 1 / f(in meters). SI Unit: Dioptre (D). Convex lens P > 0; Concave lens P < 0.
Power combination: P = P₁ + P₂.
Chapter 10: The Human Eye and the Colourful World [4 Marks Marks |
Unit III]
1. Human Eye Anatomy: Cornea (refraction window), Iris (controls pupil size), Pupil
(regulates light), Eye Lens (convex, fibrous jelly), Ciliary Muscles (modifies focal
length during accommodation), Retina (screen with rods/cones). Near point D = 25
cm; Far point = Infinity.
2. Vision Defects & Corrections:
• Myopia (Near-sightedness): Sees near objects clearly. Image forms in front of retina. Causes:
excessive lens curvature, elongated eyeball. Correction: Concave Lens.
• Hypermetropia (Far-sightedness): Sees distant objects clearly. Image forms behind retina. Causes:
lens focal length too long, short eyeball. Correction: Convex Lens.
• Presbyopia: Old-age defect due to weak ciliary muscles. Correction: Bifocal Lens.
3. Prism Dispersion & Phenomena: White light splits into VIBGYOR spectrum (Red
bends least, Violet bends most). Rainbow formation involves Dispersion, Internal
Reflection, and Refraction. Atmospheric Refraction causes twinkling of stars,
advanced sunrise (2 min), and delayed sunset (2 min). Scattering causes blue sky and
red sunrise/sunset.

Chapter 11: Electricity [7 Marks Marks | Unit IV]
1. Charge, Current & Potential: Charge Q = ne (e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C). Current I = Q / t (Unit:
Ampere A, measured in series by Ammeter). Potential Difference V = W / Q (Unit: Volt
V, measured in parallel by Voltmeter).
2. Ohm's Law & Resistance: V = I R at constant temperature. Resistance R = ρ (l / A).
Resistivity ρ depends only on material and temperature (Unit: Ω·m). Alloys have high
resistivity and don't oxidize at high temp (used in heaters/irons).
3. Resistor Networks:
• Series Combination: R_s = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ (Current identical; Voltage divides).
• Parallel Combination: 1/R_p = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ (Voltage identical; Current divides; used in
domestic wiring).
4. Joule's Heating & Power: Heat H = I² R t = V I t = (V²/R) t. Electric Power P = V I = I²
R = V²/R (Unit: Watt W). Commercial Unit of Energy: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ Joules.
Chapter 12: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current [6 Marks Marks | Unit
IV]
1. Magnetic Field & Lines: Oersted experiment proved current produces magnetic
field. Field lines emerge from North, enter South externally; closed loops; never
intersect.
2. Conductor Field Rules: Straight wire field = concentric circles. Right-Hand Thumb
Rule: Thumb = Current direction, Curled fingers = Field lines. Solenoid field is uniform
inside (like bar magnet). Electromagnet = Soft iron core inside solenoid.
3. Force & Left-Hand Rule: Force is maximum when conductor is perpendicular to
magnetic field. Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: Forefinger = Field (B), Middle finger =
Current (I), Thumb = Force/Motion (F).
4. Domestic Wiring: Live Wire (Red, 220V), Neutral Wire (Black, 0V), Earth Wire
(Green, 0V safety against shocks). 50 Hz AC supply. Short-circuiting (live and neutral
touch) & Overloading (excessive load) prevented by Electric Fuse. *Note:
Motor/Generator excluded.*
UNIT V: NATURAL RESOURCES & ENVIRONMENT (5 MARKS)
Chapter 13: Our Environment [5 Marks Marks | Unit V]
1. Ecosystem Structure: Self-sustaining unit of biotic (producers, consumers,
decomposers) and abiotic components (temp, soil, water). Natural (forest, pond) vs
Artificial (aquarium, crop field). Decomposers (bacteria/fungi) enable nutrient cycling.
2. Food Chain & 10% Energy Law: Producers capture 1% solar energy. Lindeman's 10%
Law: Only 10% energy transfers to next higher trophic level; 90% lost as metabolic
heat. Food chains limited to 3-4 steps.
3. Biological Magnification: Progressive accumulation and increasing concentration of
non-biodegradable toxic chemicals (e.g., DDT) at higher trophic levels (maximum in
human top carnivores).

UV radiation (O₂ + UV → O + O; O + O₂ → O₃). Depleted by Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs,
