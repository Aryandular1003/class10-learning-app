Impact of the First World War (1914–1918) on Indian Society & Economy:\nThe First World War created a completely new economic and political situation in India:\n* **Defense Expenditure & Taxation**: The British government increased defense expenditure, financed by war loans and increased taxes. Custom duties were raised and income tax was introduced for the first time.\n* **Price Rise & Inflation**: Prices doubled between 1913 and 1918, leading to extreme hardship for the common people.\n* **Forced Recruitment**: Rural areas were forced to supply soldiers for the British imperial army, causing widespread anger in villages.\n* **Crop Failures & Influenza Epidemic (1918–1921)**: Crop failures in 1918–19 and 1920–21 led to acute food shortages, accompanied by an influenza epidemic. According to the 1921 Census, 12 to 13 million (120–130 lakh) people perished due to famines and epidemic.\n\n#### 2. The Idea of Satyagraha:\n* **Concept**: Satyagraha emphasized the **power of truth** and the **need to search for truth**. It suggested that if the cause was true and the struggle was against injustice, physical force was not necessary to fight the oppressor.\n* **Methodology**: A Satyagrahi could win the battle through non-violence (*Ahimsa*) by appealing to the conscience of the oppressor rather than using coercion.\n* **Mahatma Gandhi's Return**: Gandhiji returned to India from South Africa in **January 1915**, where he had successfully fought racist regime with satyagraha.

#### 3. Early Local Satyagrahas led by Gandhiji (1917–1918):
1. **Champaran (Bihar, 1917)**: Inspired peasants to struggle against the oppressive indigo plantation system (*Tinkathia system*).
2. **Kheda (Gujarat, 1917)**: Supported peasants affected by crop failure and a plague epidemic who could not pay land revenue and demanded revenue relaxation.
3. **Ahmedabad (Gujarat, 1918)**: Organized a satyagraha movement among cotton mill workers demanding a 35% wage bonus.

#### 4. The Rowlatt Act (1919) & Jallianwala Bagh Massacre:
* **The Rowlatt Act (passed March 1919)**: Hurriedly passed through the Imperial Legislative Council despite united opposition from Indian members. It gave the British government enormous powers to repress political activities and allowed detention of political prisoners **without trial for up to two years**.
* **Rowlatt Satyagraha**: Gandhiji called for a nationwide satyagraha starting with a *hartal* on **6 April 1919**.
* **Jallianwala Bagh Incident (13 April 1919)**:
  * Location: Amritsar, Punjab.
  * Context: A large crowd gathered in the enclosed ground of Jallianwala Bagh to protest against the arrest of leaders (Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr. Satyapal) and to attend the annual Baisakhi fair. Many villagers were unaware of martial law imposed by **General Dyer**.
  * Execution: Dyer blocked the only exit points and opened fire on the unarmed crowd, killing hundreds. Dyer declared his objective was to *"produce a moral effect"* and strike terror in the minds of satyagrahis.
  * Aftermath: News spread, leading to strikes and clashes. Seeing violence spread, Gandhiji called off the Rowlatt Satyagraha.

#### 5. Khilafat Movement & Alliance with Non-Cooperation:
* **Background**: Ottoman Turkey was defeated in the First World War. Rumors spread that a harsh peace treaty was going to be imposed on the Ottoman Emperor—the **Khalifa** (the spiritual head of the Islamic world).
* **Khilafat Committee**: Formed in **Bombay in March 1919** by young Muslim leaders **Muhammad Ali** and **Shaukat Ali** (Ali Brothers).
* **Calcutta Congress Session (September 1920)**: Gandhiji convinced Congress leaders to support Khilafat and launch Non-Cooperation for Swaraj.
* **Nagpur Congress Session (December 1920)**: A compromise was worked out and the Non-Cooperation Programme was formally adopted.

#### 6. Why Non-Cooperation? (*Hind Swaraj*, 1909):
In his famous book *Hind Swaraj* (1909), Mahatma Gandhi declared that British rule was established in India with the cooperation of Indians, and had survived only because of this cooperation. If Indians refused to cooperate, British rule in India would collapse within a year, and Swaraj would come.

### SUB-TOPIC 2: DIFFERING STRANDS WITHIN THE NON-COOPERATION MOVEMENT

The Non-Cooperation–Khilafat Movement began in **January 1921**. Various social groups participated, each with its own specific aspiration and interpretation of *Swaraj*.

#### 1. Movement in Towns & Cities:
* **Participation**: Thousands of students left government-controlled schools and colleges; headmasters and teachers resigned; lawyers (like Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das) gave up legal practice.
* **Boycott of Foreign Goods**: Foreign cloth was burnt in huge bonfires, liquor shops were picketed, and Council elections were boycotted (except in Madras by the **Justice Party**, representing non-Brahmans).
* **Economic Impact**: Imports of foreign cloth fell by half between 1921 and 1922 (value dropped from Rs 102 crore to Rs 57 crore).
* **Slowdown Reasons**:
  1. **Khadi cloth was more expensive** than mass-produced mill cloth; poor people could not afford it for long.
  2. **Lack of alternative Indian institutions**: Students and teachers began trickling back to government schools, and lawyers rejoined government courts.

#### 2. Rebellion in the Countryside (Rural Areas):
* **Awadh Peasant Movement**:
  * **Leader**: **Baba Ramchandra** (a sanyasi who had earlier been to Fiji as an indentured laborer).
  * **Targets**: *Talukdars* and landlords who demanded excessively high rents and cesses, and forced peasants into *Begar* (unpaid forced labor).
  * **Demands**: Reduction of revenue, abolition of *begar*, and social boycott of oppressive landlords (*Nai-Dhobi bandhs*).
  * **Oudh Kisan Sabha**: Set up in **October 1920** by Jawaharlal Nehru, Baba Ramchandra, and others. Within a month, over 300 branches were set up.
  * **Deviation**: As the movement spread in 1921, houses of talukdars were attacked, bazaars looted, and grain hoards taken over.

* **Tribal Movement in Gudem Hills (Andhra Pradesh)**:
  * **Cause**: British government closed large forest areas, preventing tribals from entering forests to graze cattle or collect fuel wood and fruits, and forced them to contribute *begar* for road building.
  * **Leader**: **Alluri Sitarama Raju**:
    * Claimed special powers (astrological predictions, healing abilities, bulletproof body).
    * Inspired by Gandhiji and persuaded people to wear Khadi and give up drinking.
    * **Difference from Gandhiji**: Raju asserted that India could be liberated only by the use of **force**, not non-violence.
    * Led guerrilla warfare against police stations; Raju was captured and executed in **1924**.

#### 3. Swaraj in the Plantations (Assam):
* **Inland Emigration Act of 1859**: Plantation workers in Assam were not permitted to leave tea gardens without permission, which was rarely given.
* **Workers' Concept of Swaraj**: For plantation workers, Swaraj meant the right to move freely in and out of confined spaces and retaining a link with their native villages.
* **Action**: Thousands of workers defied authorities, left plantations, and headed home believing *Gandhi Raj* was coming. They were stranded by a railway/steamer strike and caught by police and beaten up.

### SUB-TOPIC 3: TOWARDS CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE MOVEMENT

#### 1. Withdrawal of Non-Cooperation Movement (February 1922):
* **Chauri Chaura Incident**: On **5 February 1922**, at Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur district, Uttar Pradesh), a peaceful demonstration in a bazaar turned into a violent clash with police, in which peasants set fire to a police station, killing 22 policemen.
* **Decision**: Gandhiji immediately called off the Non-Cooperation Movement, feeling satyagrahis needed to be properly trained before mass struggles.

#### 2. Internal Debates in Congress:
* **Swaraj Party (1923)**: Formed by **C.R. Das** and **Motilal Nehru** within Congress to participate in council elections and oppose British policies from within councils.
* **Young Radical Leaders**: **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Subhas Chandra Bose** pressed for more radical mass agitation and full independence.

#### 3. Two Factors Shaping Late 1920s Politics:
1. **Economic Depression**: Agricultural prices began to fall from 1926 and collapsed after 1930, creating severe distress in rural India.
2. **Simon Commission (1928)**:
  * Set up by the British Statutory Commission under Sir John Simon to review the constitutional system.
  * **Key Flaw**: It did **not have a single Indian member**; all members were British.
  * **Protest**: When it arrived in India in 1928, it was greeted with slogans: **"Go Back Simon"**. Lala Lajpat Rai was police-assaulted during a demonstration in Lahore and later succumbed to injuries.

#### 4. Demand for Purna Swaraj (1929):
* **Lahore Congress Session (December 1929)**: Presided over by **Jawaharlal Nehru**.
* **Resolution**: Passed the demand for **Purna Swaraj** (Full Independence).
* **Declaration**: **26 January 1930** was declared as Independence Day, when people were to take a pledge to struggle for complete independence.

#### 5. The Salt March (Dandi March) & Civil Disobedience Movement (1930):
* **Why Salt?**: Salt was consumed by rich and poor alike. The tax on salt and government monopoly over its production revealed the most oppressive face of British rule.
* **Ultimatum**: On 31 January 1930, Gandhiji sent a letter to Viceroy Irwin stating **11 demands**. Irwin was unwilling to negotiate.
* **Dandi March Details**:
  * **Start Date**: 12 March 1930.
  * **Route**: From Sabarmati Ashram to the coastal town of **Dandi** (Gujarat).
  * **Distance**: 240 miles, covered in 24 days (10 miles a day) with 78 trusted volunteers.
  * **Reaching Dandi**: **6 April 1930**, Gandhiji manufactured salt by boiling sea water, breaking the salt law. This marked the official launch of the **Civil Disobedience Movement**.

#### 6. Difference between Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience:
* **Non-Cooperation (1920–22)**: People were asked only **not to cooperate** with the British government.
* **Civil Disobedience (1930–34)**: People were asked not only to refuse cooperation but also to **break colonial laws** (e.g., salt law, forest laws, non-payment of land revenue/chaukidari tax).

#### 7. Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931) & Round Table Conference:
* **Pact Terms**: Gandhiji called off the movement and agreed to attend the **Second Round Table Conference** in London; Viceroy Irwin agreed to release political prisoners.
* **Outcome**: Negotiations in London broke down; Gandhiji returned disappointed. He restarted Civil Disobedience, but by 1934 it lost its momentum.

### SUB-TOPIC 4: PARTICIPATION OF SOCIAL GROUPS & LIMITS OF CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE

#### 1. How Different Social Groups Saw the Movement:

| Social Group | Prominent Figures / Organizations | Primary Grievance / Motivation | Concept of Swaraj |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rich Peasants** | Patidars of Gujarat, Jats of Uttar Pradesh | High land revenue rates during trade depression & falling prices | Struggle against high land revenue |
| **Poor Peasants** | Small tenants & agricultural laborers | Unpayable rent to landlords as cash income dwindled | "No-Rent" campaign (remission of rent) |
| **Business Class** | Purshottamdas Thakurdas, G.D. Birla; FICCI (1927), IICC (1920) | Protection against imports of foreign goods & sterling-rupee exchange ratio | Freedom for business without imperial restrictions |
| **Industrial Working Class** | Railway workers (1930), Dockworkers (1932), Chotanagpur tin miners | Low wages and poor working conditions | High wages and better working conditions (Limited participation except in Nagpur) |
| **Women** | Thousands of urban & rural women | Inspired by Gandhiji's call | Service to the nation as a sacred duty (participated in marches, salt-making, picketing) |

#### 2. Limits of Civil Disobedience:
1. **Untouchables / Dalits**:
   * For long, Congress ignored Dalits for fear of offending conservative high-caste Hindus (*Sanatanis*).
   * Gandhiji called them **Harijans** ("Children of God"), organized satyagraha for temple entry, and cleaned toilets himself.
   * **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**: Formed the **Depressed Classes Association (1930)** and demanded separate electorates for Dalits at the Second Round Table Conference.
   * **Poona Pact (September 1932)**: Signed between Gandhiji and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar. Abandoned separate electorates but gave reserved seats to Depressed Classes in provincial and central legislative councils, to be voted in general electorates.
2. **Muslim Organizations**:
   * After the decline of Non-Cooperation–Khilafat, a large section of Muslims felt alienated from Congress, which was seen as visibly linked to Hindu religious nationalist groups like the **Hindu Mahasabha**.
   * **Muhammad Ali Jinnah** (Muslim League) was willing to give up demands for separate electorates if Muslims were assured reserved seats in Central Assembly and representation in Muslim-majority provinces (Bengal & Punjab).
   * Negotiations failed at the All Parties Conference in 1928 when **M.R. Jayakar** of the Hindu Mahasabha strongly opposed compromises.

### SUB-TOPIC 5: THE SENSE OF COLLECTIVE BELONGING & QUIT INDIA MOVEMENT

Nationalism spreads when people begin to believe that they are all part of the same nation and discover unified identity through cultural processes:

#### 1. Visual Allegories – Bharat Mata (Bharat Mata):
* **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay**: Created the image of Bharat Mata and wrote **'Vande Mataram'** in the 1870s as a hymn to the motherland (later included in his novel *Anandamath*).
* **Abanindranath Tagore (1905)**: Painted his famous image of **Bharat Mata** depicted as an ascetic figure—calm, composed, divine, and spiritual.

#### 2. Revival of Indian Folklore:
* **Bengal**: **Rabindranath Tagore** collected ballads, nursery rhymes, and myths, leading the folk revival movement.
* **Madras**: **Natesa Sastri** published a massive four-volume collection of Tamil folk tales titled ***The Folklore of Southern India***, believing folklore was national literature.

#### 3. Icons and Symbols (Flags):
* **Swadeshi Flag (Bengal, 1857/1905)**: Tricolor flag (red, green, yellow) with eight lotuses representing eight provinces of British India, and a crescent moon representing Hindus and Muslims.
* **Swaraj Flag (1921)**: Designed by Mahatma Gandhi. Tricolor (red, green, white) with a **spinning wheel** (*Charkha*) in the center, representing the Gandhian ideal of self-help.

#### 4. Reinterpretation of History:
British historians portrayed Indians as backward, primitive, and incapable of governing themselves. In response, Indian historians wrote about India's glorious past achievements in art, architecture, science, mathematics, religion, and trade, urging readers to take pride in Indian history.\n\n#### 5. Quit India Movement (Quit India Movement, 1942):\n* **Context**: Failure of the Cripps Mission (1942) and World War II pressures.\n* **Resolution**: Passed by Congress Working Committee at Wardha (July 1942) and endorsed at **Bombay on 8 August 1942**.\n* **Slogan**: Gandhiji gave the famous mantra: **"Do or Die"** (*Do or die*).\n* **Nature**: A mass movement where farmers, youth, students, and workers participated. Leaders like Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, and Aruna Asaf Ali engaged in underground activities. When workers heard of the Non-Cooperation Movement, thousands defied authorities, left plantations, and headed for home, believing *Gandhi Raj* was coming.\n\n---\n\n### 2. Original Predicted Long Answer Questions for Board Exam 2027:\n\n* **Predicted Question 1**: Describe the Salt March (Dandi March) and explain why it became an effective symbol of resistance against British colonialism. [4 Marks]\n  * **Answer**:\n    1. **Selection of Salt as a Symbol**: Gandhiji found in salt a powerful symbol that could unite the nation. Salt was something consumed by the rich and the poor alike, and was an essential item of food. The tax on salt and government monopoly over its production revealed the oppressive nature of British rule.\n    2. **The Ultimatum**: On 31 January 1930, Gandhiji sent a letter to Viceroy Irwin stating 11 demands. When Irwin refused to negotiate, Gandhiji launched the Salt March.\n    3. **The Dandi March**: Accompanied by 78 trusted volunteers, Gandhiji walked over 240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi in 24 days. Thousands came to hear him along the way.\n    4. **Breaking the Law & Mass Impact**: On 6 April 1930, Gandhiji reached Dandi and ceremonially manufactured salt by boiling sea water. This broke the salt law and inaugurated the Civil Disobedience Movement across the country, where thousands manufactured salt, picketed liquor shops, and boycotted foreign goods.\n\n* **Predicted Question 2**: Analyze the role of different social groups in the Civil Disobedience Movement, highlighting why rich peasants and business classes participated. [4 Marks]\n  * **Answer**:\n    1. **Rich Peasants (Patidars & Jats)**:\n       * Being producers of commercial crops, they were hard hit by the trade depression and falling agricultural prices.\n       * As their cash income disappeared, they found it impossible to pay the government's revenue demand.
       * They became enthusiastic supporters of Civil Disobedience, organizing their communities to demand revenue reduction.
    2. **Business Classes (FICCI & Industrialists)**:
       * During WWI, Indian merchants made huge profits and wanted protection against imports of foreign goods and a favorable exchange ratio.
       * Led by G.D. Birla and Purshottamdas Thakurdas, they formed FICCI (1927) and gave financial assistance to the movement, refusing to buy or sell imported goods.

## SECTION 7: 15-QUESTION MCQ QUIZ (WITH EXPLANATORY NOTES)

1. **In which year did Mahatma Gandhi return to India from South Africa?**
   (A) 1912
   (B) 1914
   (C) 1915
   (D) 1919
   * **Answer**: **(C) 1915**
   * *Note*: Gandhiji returned to India in January 1915.

2. **Where did Mahatma Gandhi organize his first Satyagraha movement in India in 1917?**
   (A) Kheda
   (B) Champaran
   (C) Ahmedabad
   (D) Amritsar
   * **Answer**: **(B) Champaran**
   * *Note*: Champaran satyagraha in Bihar was organized for indigo planters.

3. **The Rowlatt Act of 1919 allowed the British government to detain political prisoners without trial for up to:**
   (A) 1 year
   (B) 2 years
   (C) 3 years
   (D) 5 years
   * **Answer**: **(B) 2 years**
   * *Note*: Detention without trial for up to two years was the core clause of the Rowlatt Act.

4. **Who was the British military officer responsible for the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre?**
   (A) Lord Irwin
   (B) Sir John Simon
   (C) General Dyer
   (D) Lord Curzon
   * **Answer**: **(C) General Dyer**
   * *Note*: General Dyer ordered firing on the crowd on 13 April 1919 at Amritsar.

5. **In which book did Mahatma Gandhi declare that British rule was established in India with the cooperation of Indians?**
   (A) My Experiments with Truth
   (B) Discovery of India
   (C) Hind Swaraj
   (D) Anandamath
   * **Answer**: **(C) Hind Swaraj**
   * *Note*: *Hind Swaraj* was written by Gandhiji in 1909.

6. **The Khilafat Committee was formed in March 1919 in which city?**
   (A) Calcutta
   (B) Bombay
   (C) Lucknow
   (D) Amritsar
   * **Answer**: **(B) Bombay**
   * *Note*: Formed in Bombay by Ali Brothers (Muhammad Ali & Shaukat Ali).

7. **At which Congress Session in December 1920 was the Non-Cooperation Programme formally adopted?**
   (A) Calcutta Session
   (B) Nagpur Session
   (C) Lahore Session
   (D) Madras Session
   * **Answer**: **(B) Nagpur Session**
   * *Note*: Non-cooperation was adopted at Nagpur in December 1920 after initial approval at Calcutta in Sept 1920.

8. **Who led the peasant movement in Awadh against talukdars and landlords?**
   (A) Alluri Sitarama Raju
   (B) Baba Ramchandra
   (C) Jawaharlal Nehru
   (D) Motilal Nehru
   * **Answer**: **(B) Baba Ramchandra**
   * *Note*: Baba Ramchandra was a sanyasi and former indentured laborer in Fiji.

9. **The Swaraj Party was formed within Congress in 1923 by:**
   (A) Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose
   (B) C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru
   (C) Mahatma Gandhi and Sardar Patel
   (D) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Maulana Azad
   * **Answer**: **(B) C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru**
   * *Note*: Formed to enter legislative councils and oppose British policies from within.

10. **The resolution of 'Purna Swaraj' (Full Independence) was passed at the Lahore Congress Session under the presidency of:**
    (A) Mahatma Gandhi
    (B) Jawaharlal Nehru
    (C) Subhas Chandra Bose
    (D) Sarojini Naidu
    * **Answer**: **(B) Jawaharlal Nehru**
    * *Note*: Passed in December 1929 at Lahore.

11. **How many trusted volunteers accompanied Mahatma Gandhi on the Dandi Salt March?**
    (A) 55
    (B) 78
    (C) 100
    (D) 240
    * **Answer**: **(B) 78**
    * *Note*: Gandhiji started the 240-mile march with 78 volunteers.

12. **The Depressed Classes Association was established in 1930 by:**
    (A) Mahatma Gandhi
    (B) Jyotirao Phule
    (C) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
    (D) Periyar E.V. Ramasamy
    * **Answer**: **(C) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**
    * *Note*: Dr. Ambedkar established it to demand political rights for Dalits.

13. **The Poona Pact of September 1932 resulted in:**
    (A) Withdrawal of Civil Disobedience
    (B) Reservation of seats for Depressed Classes in general electorates
    (C) Formation of Swaraj Party
    (D) Launch of Quit India Movement
    * **Answer**: **(B) Reservation of seats for Depressed Classes in general electorates**
    * *Note*: Signed between Gandhiji and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

14. **Who painted the famous image of Bharat Mata depicted as an ascetic figure in 1905?**
    (A) Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay
    (B) Rabindranath Tagore
    (C) Abanindranath Tagore
    (D) Raja Ravi Varma
    * **Answer**: **(C) Abanindranath Tagore**
    * *Note*: Abanindranath Tagore painted the iconic calm, ascetic Bharat Mata image.

15. **The slogan "Do or Die" was given by Mahatma Gandhi during which movement?**
    (A) Non-Cooperation Movement
    (B) Civil Disobedience Movement
    (C) Rowlatt Satyagraha
    (D) Quit India Movement
    * **Answer**: **(D) Quit India Movement**
    * *Note*: Given during the Quit India speech at Bombay on 8 August 1942.
