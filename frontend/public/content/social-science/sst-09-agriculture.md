## 1. Types of Farming

India is an agriculturally important country where two-thirds of the population is engaged in agricultural activities. Farming systems in India vary from subsistence to commercial types based on physical environment, technological know-how, and socio-cultural practices.

### A. Primitive Subsistence Farming
* **Definition**: Practised on small patches of land using primitive tools (hoe, dao, digging sticks) and family/community labour.
* **Key Characteristics**:
  * Depends entirely on **monsoon**, natural fertility of soil, and environmental suitability.
  * Land productivity is low as farmers do not use fertilizers or modern inputs.
* **Slash and Burn Agriculture (Shifting Cultivation / Jhumming)**:
  * Farmers clear a patch of forest vegetation, burn it, and mix ash with soil to cultivate food crops.
  * When soil fertility decreases, farmers shift to a fresh patch of land, allowing the old patch to replenish naturally through vegetative growth.

```
[Forest Patch Cleared & Burned] ──> [Crops Cultivated on Ash-Enriched Soil] ──> [Soil Fertility Declines (2-3 yrs)]
                                                                                          │
                                                                                          ▼
[Natural Soil Recovery (Abandoned)] <── [Farmers Shift to New Forest Clearing] <──────────┘
```

### B. Intensive Subsistence Farming
* **Definition**: Practised in areas of **high population pressure on land**.
* **Key Characteristics**:
  * It is **labour-intensive farming** where high doses of biochemical inputs (fertilizers, pesticides) and irrigation are applied to obtain higher yields from small land holdings.
  * **Cause of Land Pressure**: The **Right of Inheritance** leading to division of land among successive generations has rendered land-holding sizes economically unviable. Farmers attempt to extract maximum yield from limited land, creating huge pressure on agricultural land.

---

### C. Commercial Farming
* **Definition**: Farming where the main objective is to sell agricultural produce in the market for profit.
* **Key Characteristics**:
  * Use of higher doses of modern inputs, such as **High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds**, chemical fertilizers, insecticides, and pesticides to achieve higher productivity.
  * The degree of commercialization varies from region to region. For example, **Rice is a commercial crop in Punjab and Haryana**, but in Odisha, it is a **subsistence crop**.

---

### D. Plantation Agriculture
* **Definition**: A specialized type of commercial farming where a **single crop is grown on a large estate** for commercial sale.
* **Key Features**:
  * Capital-intensive inputs using modern technology and migratory labour.
  * All produce is used as a raw material in respective industries.
  * Requires an efficient transport and communication network connecting plantation estates, processing industries, and markets.
* **Major Plantation Crops in India**:
  * **Tea**: Assam and North Bengal.
  * **Coffee**: Karnataka (Nilgiri Hills, Baba Budan Hills).
  * **Rubber**: Kerala.
  * **Banana**: Southern India (Tamil Nadu, Kerala).
  * **Sugarcane**: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra.

## 2. Cropping Pattern and Cropping Seasons in India

India has three distinct cropping seasons: **Rabi**, **Kharif**, and **Zaid**.

                                                │
         ┌──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                      ▼                                      ▼
  ┌──────────────┐                       ┌──────────────┐                       ┌──────────────┐
  │  RABI SEASON │                       │KHARIF SEASON │                       │ ZAID SEASON  │
  ├──────────────┤                       ├──────────────┤                       ├──────────────┤
  │Sow: Oct-Dec  │                       │Sow: Jun-Jul  │                       │Sow: Mar-Apr  │
  │Harv: Apr-Jun │                       │Harv: Sep-Oct │                       │Harv: May-Jun │
  │E.g. Wheat,   │                       │E.g. Paddy,   │                       │E.g. Water-   │
  │Barley, Gram  │                       │Maize, Bajra  │                       │melon, Cucumber│
  └──────────────┘                       └──────────────┘                       └──────────────┘
```

| Cropping Season | Sowing Period | Harvesting Period | Major Crops Grown | Key Characteristics & Regions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rabi** | Winter (October to December) | Summer (April to June) | **Wheat**, **Barley**, **Peas**, **Gram**, **Mustard** | Availability of precipitation during winter months from Western Temperate Cyclones helps in crop growth. Key states: Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh. |
| **Kharif** | Onset of Monsoon (June to July) | Autumn (September to October) | **Paddy (Rice)**, **Maize**, **Jowar**, **Bajra**, **Tur (Arhar)**, **Moong**, **Urad**, **Cotton**, **Jute**, **Groundnut**, **Soyabean** | Grown with the arrival of monsoon rains. Rice has become an important crop in Punjab & Haryana due to canal irrigation & tube wells. In Assam, WB, and Odisha, 3 crops of paddy are grown in a year: **Aus**, **Aman**, and **Boro**. |
| **Zaid** | Short summer season (March to June) | Before Kharif monsoon (May to June) | **Watermelon**, **Muskmelon**, **Cucumber**, **Vegetables**, **Fodder crops** | A short season between Rabi and Kharif during summer months. Sugarcane takes almost a full year to grow. |

## 3. Major Crops in India – Food Grains

### A. Rice (धान / चावल)
* **Importance**: The staple food crop of a majority of the people in India. India is the **second-largest producer** of rice in the world after China.
* **Geographical Requirements**:
  * **Crop Type**: Kharif crop.
  * **Temperature**: High temperature (**above 25°C**).
  * **Humidity & Rainfall**: High humidity with annual rainfall **above 100 cm**. In areas of less rainfall (Punjab, Haryana, Western UP), it grows with the help of irrigation.
  * **Soil**: Deep clayey or loamy alluvial soil capable of retaining moisture.
* **Major Producing Regions**: Plains of north and north-eastern India, coastal areas, and deltaic regions. Development of dense canal network and tube wells has made it possible to grow rice in Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh, and parts of Rajasthan.

---

### B. Wheat (गेहूँ)
* **Importance**: The second most important cereal crop in India. It is the main food crop in the north and north-western parts of the country.
* **Geographical Requirements**:
  * **Crop Type**: Rabi crop.
  * **Temperature**: Cool growing season and **bright sunshine** at the time of ripening.
  * **Rainfall**: **50 to 75 cm** of annual rainfall evenly distributed over the growing season. Winter rainfall caused by Western Cyclonic Disturbances is beneficial.
  * **Soil**: Well-drained fertile loamy and clayey soils (Alluvial soil of Northern Plains and Black soil of Deccan).
* **Major Producing Zones**:
  1. The **Ganga-Satluj plains** in the north-west.
  2. The **Black soil region** of the Deccan.
* **Leading States**: Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Bihar.

---

### C. Millets / Coarse Grains (मोटे अनाज)
Millets have very high nutritional value. The three major millets grown in India are **Jowar**, **Bajra**, and **Ragi**.

1. **Jowar (ज्वार)**:
   * **Importance**: Third most important food crop with respect to area and production.
   * **Requirements**: Rain-fed crop mostly grown in moist areas which hardly needs irrigation.
   * **Leading States**: **Maharashtra** (largest producer), Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh.
2. **Bajra (बाजरा)**:
   * **Requirements**: Grows well on sandy soils and shallow black soil.
   * **Leading States**: **Rajasthan** (largest producer), Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Haryana.
3. **Ragi (रागी)**:
   * **Importance**: Extremely rich in **iron, calcium**, other micro-nutrients, and roughage.
   * **Requirements**: Crop of dry regions; grows well on red, black, sandy, loamy, and shallow black soils.
   * **Leading States**: **Karnataka** (largest producer), Tamil Nadu, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim.

---

### D. Maize (मक्का)
* **Importance**: Used both as **food and fodder**.
* **Geographical Requirements**:
  * **Crop Type**: Primarily a Kharif crop (in Bihar, it is also grown in Rabi season).
  * **Temperature**: **21°C to 27°C**.
  * **Soil**: Grows well in **old alluvial soil** (*Bhangar*).
  * **Inputs**: Use of modern inputs such as HYV seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation has contributed to increasing maize production.
* **Leading States**: Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana.

---

### E. Pulses (दाले)
* **Importance**: India is the **largest producer as well as the largest consumer** of pulses in the world. Pulses are the major source of protein in a vegetarian diet.
* **Major Pulses**: Tur (Arhar), Urad, Moong, Masur, Peas, Gram.
* **Key Features**:
  * Need less moisture and survive even in dry conditions.
  * **Leguminous Crops**: Except Tur (Arhar), all pulses help in **restoring soil fertility by fixing atmospheric nitrogen** in the soil. Therefore, they are mostly grown in **rotation with other crops**.
* **Leading States**: Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka.

---

## 4. Food Crops Other than Grains

### A. Sugarcane (गन्ना)
* **Nature**: Tropical as well as a subtropical crop. India is the **second-largest producer** after Brazil.
* **Geographical Requirements**:
  * **Temperature**: Hot and humid climate with temperature between **21°C and 27°C**.
  * **Rainfall**: Annual rainfall between **75 cm and 100 cm**. Irrigation is required in regions of low rainfall.
  * **Soil**: Can be grown on a variety of soils; requires manual labour from sowing to harvesting.
* **Products**: Main source of sugar, gur (jaggery), khandsari, and molasses.
* **Leading States**: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana.

---

### B. Oilseeds (तिलहन)
* **Importance**: India produces about 12% of the world's oilseeds. Used as cooking medium, raw material in soap, cosmetics, and ointments.
* **Major Oilseeds Grown**: Groundnut, Mustard, Coconut, Sesame, Soyabean, Castor seed, Cottonseed, Linseed, Sunflower.
* **Key Crop Profiles**:
  * **Groundnut (मूंगफली)**: Kharif crop; accounts for half of major oilseeds produced in India. **Gujarat** is the leading producer, followed by Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu.
  * **Mustard (सरसों)**: Rabi crop.
  * **Sesamum (तिल)**: Kharif crop in north India, Rabi crop in south India.
  * **Castor Seed (अरंडी)**: Grown both as Rabi and Kharif crop.

---

### C. Beverage Crops: Tea & Coffee

#### 1. Tea (चाय)
* **Nature**: Plantation agriculture; important beverage crop introduced initially by the British in India.
* **Geographical Requirements**:
  * **Climate**: Tropical and subtropical climate with **warm and moist frost-free climate** all through the year.
  * **Rainfall**: Frequent showers (**150 to 200 cm**) evenly distributed over the year ensure continuous growth of tender leaves.
  * **Soil**: Deep, fertile, well-drained soil rich in **humus and organic matter**. Stagnant water damages roots; grown on hill slopes.
  * **Labour**: **Labour-intensive industry**; requires abundant, cheap, and skilled labour.
* **Leading Producing Zones**: Assam, West Bengal (Darjeeling and Jalpaiguri districts), Tamil Nadu, Kerala.

#### 2. Coffee (कॉफ़ी)
* **Varietal Specialty**: Indian coffee is known for its good quality. The **Arabica variety** brought from **Yemen** is produced in India and is in great demand worldwide.
* **Cultivation Region**: Initially introduced on the **Baba Budan Hills**; today its cultivation is confined to the **Nilgiri Hills** in Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu.

---

### D. Horticulture Crops (बागवानी फ़सलें)
* **Definition**: Cultivation of fruits and vegetables. India produces both tropical and temperate fruits.
* **Famous Fruit Hubs of India**:
  * **Mangoes**: Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal.
  * **Oranges**: Nagpur and Cherrapunjee (Meghalaya).
  * **Bananas**: Kerala, Mizoram, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu.
  * **Lichi and Guava**: Uttar Pradesh, Bihar.
  * **Pineapples**: Meghalaya.
  * **Grapes**: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra.
  * **Apples, Pears, Walnuts, Peaches**: Jammu & Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh.
* **Vegetables**: India produces about 13% of the world's vegetables (Pea, Cauliflower, Onion, Cabbage, Tomato, Brinjal, Potato).

## 5. Non-Food Crops & Fibre Crops

### A. Rubber (रबड़)
* **Equatorial Crop**: Grown in tropical and subtropical areas.
* **Geographical Requirements**:
  * **Climate**: Moist and humid climate.
  * **Rainfall & Temp**: Rainfall of **more than 200 cm** and temperature **above 25°C**.
* **Major Producing States**: Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Garo hills of Meghalaya.

---

### B. Fibre Crops: Cotton & Jute

#### 1. Cotton (कपास)
* **Importance**: India is believed to be the original home of the cotton plant. Main raw material for the cotton textile industry.
* **Geographical Requirements**:
  * **Crop Type**: Kharif crop; requires **6 to 8 months** to mature.
  * **Soil**: Grows well in drier parts of the **Black Cotton Soil** (*Regur Soil*) of the Deccan Plateau.
  * **Temperature & Sunshine**: High temperature, light rainfall or irrigation, **210 frost-free days**, and **bright sunshine** for growth.
* **Leading States**: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Haryana, UP.

#### 2. Jute – The "Golden Fibre" (जूट / सुनहरा रेशा)
* **Soil Requirements**: Grows well on well-drained fertile soils in the **flood plains** where soils are renewed every year.
* **Temperature**: High temperature during the time of growth.
* **Uses**: Gunny bags, mats, ropes, yarn, carpets, and artifacts.
* **Major Producing States**: West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha, Meghalaya.
* **Market Challenge**: Losing market to **synthetic fibres** and packing materials (nylon) due to high cost.

## 6. Technological and Institutional Reforms in Indian Agriculture

Agriculture provides livelihood for more than 60% of India's population. Sustained uses of land without compatible techno-institutional changes have hindered agricultural development. Therefore, comprehensive reforms were implemented post-independence.

```

### A. Institutional Reforms
1. **Collectivisation and Consolidation of Holdings (*Chakbandi*)**: Fragmentation of land holdings due to succession laws was resolved through land consolidation.
2. **Abolition of Zamindari System**: Intermediaries between government and farmers were removed, transferring land ownership to actual tillers.
3. **Land Reform Legislation**: Primary focus of the **First Five-Year Plan**.
4. **Provision of Crop Insurance**: Protection against drought, flood, cyclone, fire, and disease.
5. **Establishment of Rural Credit Networks**: **Grameen Banks**, cooperative societies, and bank branches set up to provide loans at lower interest rates.
6. **Kisan Credit Card (KCC) and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS)**: Introduced for easy credit access and financial security of farmers.
7. **Minimum Support Price (MSP)**: Announcement of procurement and remunerative prices for important crops to check exploitation of farmers by speculators and middlemen.
8. **Agricultural Broadcasting**: Special weather bulletins and agricultural programmes broadcasted for farmers on Radio and Television.

---

### B. Technological Reforms
1. **Green Revolution**: Introduced in the late 1960s based on the use of **package technology**—HYV seeds, chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and canal irrigation.
2. **White Revolution (Operation Flood)**: Strategies to increase milk production across India.
3. **Modern Inputs & Mechanisation**: Replacement of wooden ploughs with iron ploughs, tractors, harvesters, and drip/sprinkler irrigation systems.

---

### C. Bhoodan – Gramdan Movement ("Bloodless Revolution")
* **Initiator**: **Vinoba Bhave**, spiritual heir of Mahatma Gandhi.
* **Background**: During a padayatra at **Pochampalli** (Andhra Pradesh), poor landless villagers demanded land for economic well-being.
* **Bhoodan**: **Shri Ram Chandra Reddy** offered 80 acres of land to be distributed among 80 landless villagers. This act was known as **Bhoodan** (donation of land).
* **Gramdan**: Zamindars and owners of many villages offered to distribute entire villages among landless farmers; known as **Gramdan**.
* **Historical Significance**: Known as the **"Bloodless Revolution"** in Indian history as land reform was achieved through voluntary land donation without violence.

# SECTION 6: VERIFIED PREVIOUS-YEAR BOARD QUESTIONS (PYQs)

### Question 1 (BSER 2026–27 Official Model Paper – Q28)
**Question**: "In India, intensive subsistence agriculture is practiced in regions where land is limited and population pressure is high. In such areas, farmers employ modern techniques, irrigation and fertilizers to maximize production. If you were an agricultural policymaker, provide three practical suggestions to make intensive subsistence agriculture more sustainable, environmentally friendly, and beneficial to small farmers. In your answer consider the interests of all three stakeholders: farmers, natural resources and society." **[3 Marks]**

**Model Answer**:
If I were an agricultural policymaker, I would recommend the following three practical steps:
1. **Promote Integrated Organic Farming & Bio-Fertilizers**: Replace excessive chemical fertilizers with organic manure, vermicomposting, and bio-pesticides. This restores long-term soil health, protects groundwater from chemical leaching, and lowers input costs for small farmers.
2. **Adopt Micro-Irrigation & Water Management**: Incentivise drip and sprinkler irrigation systems (*Per Drop More Crop*) under Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana. This conserves depleted groundwater reserves while ensuring uniform crop watering.
3. **Encourage Crop Rotation & Cooperative Farming**: Promote rotation with nitrogen-fixing leguminous pulses to naturalize soil fertility, and organize small farmers into Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs) for collective machinery leasing and direct market access.

### Question 2 (BSER 2024 Main Examination Paper – Q17)
**Question**: "Describe the technological and institutional reforms of agricultural development in India." **[3 Marks]**  
*OR*  
"Describe the cropping pattern in India." **[3 Marks]**

**Model Answer**:

**(First Option: Technological and Institutional Reforms)**
To transform traditional Indian agriculture, the government introduced several key reforms:
1. **Institutional Reforms**: Collectivisation and consolidation of holdings (*Chakbandi*) eliminated land fragmentation. The Zamindari system was abolished to give ownership rights to actual tillers.
2. **Credit & Price Support**: Kisan Credit Card (KCC) and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS) were launched. Minimum Support Price (MSP) was introduced to safeguard farmers against market fluctuations.
3. **Technological Reforms**: Introduction of Green Revolution (HYV seeds, chemical fertilizers, canal/tube well irrigation) and White Revolution (Operation Flood). Weather forecasts and specialized farming bulletins were broadcasted via radio and TV.

**(Second Option: Cropping Pattern in India)**
India has three distinct cropping seasons:
1. **Rabi Season**: Sown in winter from October to December and harvested in summer from April to June. Major crops include Wheat, Barley, Peas, Gram, and Mustard.
2. **Kharif Season**: Sown with the onset of monsoon in June–July and harvested in September–October. Major crops include Paddy (Rice), Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, and Jute.
3. **Zaid Season**: A short summer season between March and June. Major crops include Watermelon, Muskmelon, Cucumber, vegetables, and fodder crops.

**Model Answer**: Organic agriculture is a holistic farming system that avoids the use of synthetic chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and genetically modified seeds. It relies on crop rotation, green manure, compost, and biological pest control. Organic farming maintains soil fertility, protects the natural environment, and produces healthy, chemical-free food crops.

### Question 4 (BSER 2024 Supplementary D&D Paper – Q22 & Q1-vii)
**Question 4A (Q22)**: "What should be the geographical conditions for wheat cultivation?" **[3 Marks]**  
**Model Answer**: Wheat is the second most important cereal crop in India. Its required geographical conditions are:
1. **Temperature**: Cool growing season during winter and bright warm sunshine at the time of ripening.
2. **Rainfall**: Requires **50 to 75 cm** of annual rainfall evenly distributed throughout the growing season. Winter showers caused by Western Disturbances are highly beneficial.
3. **Soil**: Requires deep, well-drained fertile loamy soils or clayey alluvial soil (as found in the Ganga-Satluj plains and Deccan black soil region).

# SECTION 7: PRACTICE QUESTIONS & ORIGINAL 2027 PREDICTED QUESTIONS

### Practice Question 1 (Short Answer - 2 Marks)
**Question**: Differentiate between Primitive Subsistence Farming and Intensive Subsistence Farming.  
**Model Answer**:
* **Primitive Subsistence Farming**: Practised on small land patches using basic tools (hoe, dao) and family labour. Relies entirely on monsoon and natural soil fertility. Yield per hectare is low.
* **Intensive Subsistence Farming**: Practised on small land holdings in areas of high population density. Uses high doses of biochemical inputs, fertilizers, and irrigation to maximize yield per hectare.

### Practice Question 2 (Short Answer - 2 Marks)
**Question**: Explain the Bhoodan-Gramdan movement and state why it is called the "Bloodless Revolution".  
**Model Answer**: The Bhoodan-Gramdan movement was initiated by Vinoba Bhave. When landless peasants in Pochampalli demanded land, Shri Ram Chandra Reddy voluntarily donated 80 acres of land (Bhoodan). Later, several zamindars donated entire villages to landless farmers (Gramdan). Since large-scale land redistribution was achieved voluntarily through peaceful means without bloodshed or violence, it is called the "Bloodless Revolution".

### Original 2027 Predicted Question 1 (3 Marks - High Probability)
**Question**: "Geographical conditions dictate crop distribution across India. Compare the geographical requirements of Rice and Cotton with respect to temperature, rainfall, soil type, and growing period."  
**Marking Scheme**:
* **Rice Profile (1.5 Marks)**: High temperature (>25°C), high rainfall (>100 cm), clayey/loamy alluvial soil, Kharif season.
* **Cotton Profile (1.5 Marks)**: High temperature, light rainfall (50-100 cm), 210 frost-free days with bright sunshine, Black (*Regur*) soil, requires 6-8 months.

**Model Answer**:
1. **Rice**:
   * **Temperature**: High temperature (**above 25°C**).
   * **Rainfall**: High rainfall (**above 100 cm**); requires flooded fields or canal irrigation.
   * **Soil**: Deep clayey or loamy alluvial soil capable of retaining water.
   * **Season**: Kharif crop.
2. **Cotton**:
   * **Temperature**: High temperature with **210 frost-free days** and bright sunshine.
   * **Rainfall**: Light to moderate rainfall (**50 to 100 cm**).
   * **Soil**: Drier parts of **Black Cotton Soil** (*Regur Soil*) of the Deccan Plateau.
   * **Growing Period**: Requires 6 to 8 months to mature.

### Original 2027 Predicted Question 2 (3 Marks - Analytical)
**Question**: "Why are pulses predominantly grown in crop rotation systems in India? Name the leading pulse-producing states and explain their ecological significance."  
**Marking Scheme**:
* **Ecological Significance (1.5 Marks)**: Leguminous crops fix atmospheric nitrogen, restoring soil fertility naturally; require minimal moisture.
* **Production Context & States (1.5 Marks)**: India is largest producer/consumer; leading states: MP, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, UP.

**Model Answer**:
1. **Ecological Significance**: Pulses are **leguminous crops** (except Arhar/Tur). They have root nodules containing nitrogen-fixing bacteria that convert atmospheric nitrogen into soil nitrates, thereby naturally restoring soil fertility.
2. **Low Water Requirement**: Pulses require minimal moisture and can survive in dry, semi-arid conditions.
3. **Crop Rotation**: Because pulses enrich soil nitrogen naturally, farmers grow them in rotation between heavy-feeding grain crops (like wheat or rice) to maintain soil health without chemical fertilizers.
4. **Leading States**: Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, and Karnataka.

# SECTION 8: 15-QUESTION MCQ QUIZ WITH EXPLANATORY RATIONALE

#### Q1. Slash and burn agriculture is known as 'Podu' or 'Penda' in which Indian state?
(A) Assam  
(B) Andhra Pradesh  
(C) Rajasthan  
(D) Jharkhand  
**Correct Answer**: **(B) Andhra Pradesh**

#### Q2. Which cropping season in India coincides with the arrival of monsoon rains?
(A) Rabi  
(B) Zaid  
(C) Kharif  
(D) Winter  
**Correct Answer**: **(C) Kharif**  
*Rationale*: Kharif crops are sown in June–July with the onset of the southwest monsoon.

#### Q3. In which state are three crops of paddy—Aus, Aman, and Boro—grown in a single agricultural year?
(A) Punjab  
(B) Rajasthan  
(C) West Bengal  
(D) Gujarat  
**Correct Answer**: **(C) West Bengal**  
*Rationale*: In West Bengal, Assam, and Odisha, three crops of paddy are grown annually due to favorable water and climate conditions.

#### Q4. What is the annual rainfall requirement for successful rice cultivation without intensive irrigation?
(A) 20 cm to 40 cm  
(B) 50 cm to 75 cm  
(C) Above 100 cm  
(D) Below 30 cm  
**Correct Answer**: **(C) Above 100 cm**  
*Rationale*: Rice is a water-intensive Kharif crop requiring annual rainfall exceeding 100 cm.

#### Q5. Which state is the largest producer of Bajra in India?
(A) Karnataka  
(B) Rajasthan  
(C) West Bengal  
(D) Kerala  
**Correct Answer**: **(B) Rajasthan**  
*Rationale*: Bajra grows well on sandy soils, making Rajasthan the leading producer in India.

#### Q6. Which legume crop does NOT help in fixing atmospheric nitrogen in the soil?
(A) Gram  
(B) Moong  
(C) Tur (Arhar)  
(D) Urad  
**Correct Answer**: **(C) Tur (Arhar)**  
*Rationale*: According to the NCERT textbook, except for Tur (Arhar), all pulse crops fix atmospheric nitrogen to restore soil fertility.

#### Q7. Which crop requires 210 frost-free days and bright sunshine for its growth?
(A) Jute  
(B) Cotton  
(C) Sugarcane  
(D) Tea  
**Correct Answer**: **(B) Cotton**  
*Rationale*: Cotton requires high temperature, light rainfall, 210 frost-free days, and bright sunshine to mature.

#### Q8. Arabica variety of coffee was initially brought to India from which country?
(A) Brazil  
(B) Yemen  
(C) Vietnam  
(D) Sri Lanka  
**Correct Answer**: **(B) Yemen**  
*Rationale*: The high-quality Arabica variety was brought from Yemen and initially cultivated on the Baba Budan Hills.

#### Q9. Which crop is known as the "Golden Fibre"?
(A) Cotton  
(B) Hemp  
(C) Jute  
(D) Silk  
**Correct Answer**: **(C) Jute**  
*Rationale*: Jute is traditionally known as the "Golden Fibre" due to its cash value and golden-brown natural color.

#### Q10. What was the main objective of the Bhoodan movement led by Vinoba Bhave?
(A) Imposing heavy tax on zamindars  
(B) Voluntary redistribution of land to landless farmers  
(C) Mechanisation of handloom weaving  
(D) Construction of large multi-purpose dams  
**Correct Answer**: **(B) Voluntary redistribution of land to landless farmers**  
*Rationale*: Vinoba Bhave's Bhoodan movement persuaded landowners to donate land voluntarily for landless peasants.

#### Q11. Which Indian state pioneer enacted mandatory rooftop rainwater harvesting alongside agricultural water conservation policies?
(A) Rajasthan  
(B) Tamil Nadu  
(C) Punjab  
(D) Gujarat  
**Correct Answer**: **(B) Tamil Nadu**  
*Rationale*: Tamil Nadu is the first Indian state to make rooftop rainwater harvesting compulsory by law.

#### Q12. "Operation Flood" is associated with which major agricultural sector development?
(A) Grain Production  
(B) Fish Production  
(C) Milk Production (White Revolution)  
(D) Oilseed Production  
**Correct Answer**: **(C) Milk Production (White Revolution)**  
*Rationale*: Operation Flood was launched to create a nation-wide milk grid and boost milk production in India.

#### Q13. Ragi is exceptionally rich in which micro-nutrients?
(A) Iodine and Sodium  
(B) Calcium and Iron  
(C) Vitamin C and Potassium  
(D) Fat and Sugar  
**Correct Answer**: **(B) Calcium and Iron**  
*Rationale*: Ragi is highly nutritious, containing high amounts of iron, calcium, other micro-nutrients, and roughage.

#### Q14. What type of farming is characterized by large capital investment, single-crop production, and reliance on migratory labor?
(A) Primitive Subsistence Farming  
(B) Intensive Subsistence Farming  
(C) Plantation Agriculture  
(D) Shifting Cultivation  
**Correct Answer**: **(C) Plantation Agriculture**  
*Rationale*: Plantation agriculture involves single-crop farming on large estates using capital-intensive inputs and migratory labor.

#### Q15. In which region of Rajasthan is slash and burn agriculture locally called 'Valre' or 'Waltre'?
(A) North-Western Desert Region  
(B) South-Eastern Rajasthan  
(C) Shekhawati Region

**Correct Answer**: **(B) South-Eastern Rajasthan**  
*Rationale*: In south-eastern districts of Rajasthan, shifting cultivation is traditionally known as Valre or Waltre.
