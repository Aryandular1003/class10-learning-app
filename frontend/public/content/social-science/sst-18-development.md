## Part 2: Complete Syllabus-Aligned Revision Notes

### Section 1: What Development Promises — Different People, Different Goals
* **Concept of Development**: Development involves progress, improvement, and the fulfillment of aspirations for a better life. It encompasses economic growth as well as social, health, and environmental improvements.
* **Different People, Different Development Goals**:
  * Different individuals have distinct life situations, needs, and aspirations. Therefore, their ideas of development differ.
  * *Examples*:
    * **Landless Rural Labourer**: Desires more days of work, better wages, quality local education for children, no social discrimination, and a opportunity to become leaders in the village.
    * **Prosperous Farmer from Punjab**: Desires assured high family income through higher support prices for crops, hardworking and cheap labourers, and ability to settle children abroad.
    * **Farmers Depending Only on Rain**: Desires timely monsoons, canal irrigation facilities, and cheap agricultural credit.
    * **An Urban Unemployed Youth**: Desires stable job opportunities, fair salary, and skill training.
    * **A Girl from a Rich Urban Family**: Desires as much freedom as her brother, independence in choosing her career/studies, and ability to pursue education abroad.
* **Two Core Generalisations Regarding Development**:
  1. **Different people can have different development goals.**
  2. **What may be development for one may NOT be development for the other; it may even be destructive for the other.**
     * *Example (Dam Construction)*: Industrialists and urban residents desire large dams to generate electricity for factories and homes. However, constructing large dams submerges agricultural land, displaces local tribal communities and villagers, and disrupts river ecosystems.

---

### Section 2: Income and Other Goals
* **Income as a Primary Goal**:
  * People desire higher income (regular work, better wages, decent price for crops/products) because money buys material goods and services essential for survival and comfort.
* **Non-Income Goals (Quality of Life)**:
  * Income alone does not ensure a good life. People also value non-material goals such as:
    * **Equal treatment** and non-discrimination
    * **Freedom** and personal autonomy
    * **Security** of life and job
    * **Respect** from others and self-dignity
    * **Pollution-free environment** and safe working conditions
* **Mix of Goals**:
  * Development goals are a **mix of goals** combining both income and non-income attributes.
  * *Example (Job Choice)*: Before accepting a job offer in a far-off place, a person considers non-income factors (job security, working atmosphere, travel time, family facilities, safety for women) alongside the offered salary.

### Section 3: National Development & How to Compare Countries or States
* **National Development**: A country's overall progress in improving the living standards, health, literacy, infrastructure, and equality of its citizens.
* **Comparing Countries Using Per Capita Income**:
  * To compare the development level of different countries, total income is not suitable because countries have different population sizes.
  * Therefore, we use **Average Income** or **Per Capita Income**.
$$	ext{Per Capita Income} = rac{	ext{Total Income of the Country}}{	ext{Total Population of the Country}}$$

#### World Bank Classification (World Development Reports)
* The World Bank uses **Per Capita Income** (in US Dollars adjusted for Purchasing Power Parity - PPP) as the sole criterion to classify countries:
  * **High Income / Rich Countries**: Per Capita Income of **US\$ 66,500 per annum and above** (as per recent WDR data).
  * **Low-Income Countries**: Per Capita Income of **US\$ 2,300 per annum or less**.
  * **Low Middle-Income Countries (India's Category)**: India's Per Capita Income is about **US\$ 9,047 / US\$ 11,000 per annum** (2023–24 figures), placing it in the low middle-income group.
* **Limitations of Using Average Income / Per Capita Income**:
  1. **Hides Income Disparities**: Average income tells us what an average citizen earns, but it does not tell us how income is distributed among citizens.
     * *Illustration*:
       * Country A: Incomes of 5 citizens = ₹9,500, ₹10,500, ₹9,800, ₹10,000, ₹10,200 (Average = ₹10,000). Equitable distribution; no extremely rich or extremely poor.
       * Country B: Incomes of 5 citizens = ₹500, ₹500, ₹500, ₹500, ₹48,000 (Average = ₹10,000). Highly unequal; 4 citizens are extremely poor while 1 is extremely rich.
       * Both countries have identical average income (₹10,000), but Country A is far more desirable.
  2. **Ignores Non-Material Attributes**: It excludes crucial indicators such as health status, literacy levels, infant mortality, clean air, life expectancy, and social equality.

### Section 4: Income and Other Criteria — Comparing Indian States
When comparing Indian states using per capita income alongside health and educational indicators, striking disparities emerge.

#### Comparative Data: Haryana, Kerala, and Bihar

| Indicator | Haryana | Kerala | Bihar |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Per Capita Income (2023–24)** | **₹ 3,25,759** | ₹ 2,81,001 | ₹ 60,337 |
| **Infant Mortality Rate (IMR per 1,000 live births, 2020)** | 28 | **6** | 27 |
| **Literacy Rate (%) (2017–18)** | 82% | **94%** | 62% |
| **Net Attendance Ratio (Secondary stage 15–17 yrs, 2017–18)** | 73 | **94** | 69 |

#### Analysis of State Data
1. **Per Capita Income Comparison**: Haryana ranks highest in income (₹3,25,759), while Kerala is second (₹2,81,001), and Bihar lags at the bottom (₹60,337).
2. **Health and Education Paradox**:
   * Even though Haryana has a higher per capita income than Kerala, **Kerala performs vastly better in crucial human indicators**:
     * Kerala's Infant Mortality Rate (6) is nearly **4.5 times lower** than Haryana's (28).
     * Kerala's Literacy Rate (94%) is significantly higher than Haryana's (82%) and Bihar's (62%).
     * Kerala's Net Attendance Ratio (94) demonstrates excellent school retention compared to Haryana (73) and Bihar (69).
3. **Conclusion**: Money in your pocket cannot buy all the goods and services required for a good life (e.g., pollution-free environment, unadulterated medicine, disease prevention).

### Section 5: Public Facilities & Health-Nutritional Metrics
* **Why Public Facilities are Essential**:
  * Collective provision of goods and services is much cheaper and more effective than individual provision.
  * *Examples*:
    * A village or community security guard is cheaper than every household hiring its own guard.
    * A public school allows all neighborhood children to study, whereas individual private tutoring is unaffordable for many.
* **Key Public Facilities**:
  1. **Public Distribution System (PDS)**: Ration shops providing subsidized foodgrains to low-income families. States with well-functioning PDS (e.g., Tamil Nadu, Kerala) maintain superior nutritional standards.
  2. **Public Health Centers (PHCs)**: Immunization programs, maternal healthcare, and epidemic prevention.
  3. **Basic School Infrastructure**: Free primary/secondary education reducing dropout rates.
* **Body Mass Index (BMI) — Assessing Nutritional Status**:
  * BMI is an international standard used by adult nutritionists to evaluate whether an adult is undernourished, normal, or overweight.
$$	ext{BMI} = rac{	ext{Weight in Kilograms (kg)}}{(	ext{Height in Meters (m)})^2}$$
  * **Evaluation Benchmark**:
    * **BMI < 18.5 kg/m²**: Undernourished.
    * **BMI 18.5 – 24.9 kg/m²**: Normal / Healthy weight.
    * **BMI ≥ 25.0 kg/m²**: Overweight / Obese.
  * *Survey Finding*: In India, about 20% of males and 23% of females are undernourished despite national foodgrain self-sufficiency, pointing to distribution gaps and poverty.

### Section 6: Human Development Report (UNDP vs. World Bank)

* **Human Development Index (HDI)**:
  * Published annually by the **United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)** in the *Human Development Report*.
  * Compares countries on a broad, multi-dimensional basis rather than income alone.
* **Three Core Pillars of HDI**:
  1. **Health / Longevity**: Measured by **Life Expectancy at Birth** (average expected lifespan of a newborn).
  2. **Education / Knowledge**: Measured by **Mean Years of Schooling** (for adults aged 25+) and **Expected Years of Schooling**.
  3. **Standard of Living / Income**: Measured by **Gross National Income (GNI) Per Capita** in US Dollars adjusted for Purchasing Power Parity (PPP \$).

#### Human Development Data: India and Neighbors (2023–25 Reports)

| Country | GNI Per Capita (2021 PPP \$) | Life Expectancy at Birth (years) | Mean Years of Schooling (25+ yrs) | HDI Rank in the World (out of 193) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Sri Lanka** | \$ 12,616 | 77.5 | 10.8 | **89** |
| **India** | **\$ 9,047** | **72.0** | **6.9** | **130** |
| **Bangladesh** | \$ 8,498 | 74.7 | 6.8 | **130** |
| **Nepal** | \$ 4,726 | 70.4 | 4.5 | 145 |
| **Myanmar** | \$ 4,919 | 66.9 | 6.4 | 150 |
| **Pakistan** | \$ 5,501 | 67.6 | 4.3 | 168 |

* **Key Takeaways**:
  * **Sri Lanka**, a small island neighbor, outperforms India significantly across all parameters (Rank 89 vs. India's Rank 130).
  * **Bangladesh and Nepal** have lower per capita income than India, yet Bangladesh boasts a higher Life Expectancy (74.7 years) than India (72.0 years).

#### Comparison: World Bank vs. UNDP Criteria

| Feature | World Bank Criterion | UNDP Criterion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Basis** | Single Metric: Per Capita Income (US \$ PPP) | Multi-Dimensional: Income + Health + Education |
| **Scope** | Narrow / Materialistic | Comprehensive / Quality of Life |
| **Report Title** | *World Development Report (WDR)* | *Human Development Report (HDR)* |
| **Classification** | Rich, Low-Middle, Low-Income | Human Development Index (HDI) Ranks |

### Section 7: Sustainability of Development
* **Definition of Sustainable Development**: Development that meets the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
* **Why Sustainability is Crucial**:
  * Development that depletes natural resources or degrades the environment cannot last. Future generations will face ecological collapse and resource shortages.
* **Two Key Case Studies**:

#### 1. Groundwater Overuse in India (Renewable Resource)
* Groundwater is a **renewable resource** replenished by rainfall.
* *Problem*: Overuse occurs when extraction exceeds natural recharge rates.
* *Current Crisis*: About **one-third of India** is overusing its groundwater reserves. Over 300 districts report a groundwater decline of over 4 meters in the last 20 years.
* *Affected Regions*: Agriculturally prosperous regions of Punjab and Western UP, hard-rock plateau areas of Central and South India, coastal belts, and rapidly expanding urban areas.

#### 2. Depletion of Crude Oil Reserves (Non-Renewable Resource)
* Crude oil is a **non-renewable resource** with a fixed stock created over millions of years.
* *Current Crisis*: World crude oil reserves are estimated to last only around 50 years at current consumption rates.
* *Economic & Geopolitical Vulnerability*: Countries like India, which import nearly 80–85% of their crude oil requirements, face high import bills, inflation, and economic risk whenever international prices surge.

* **Key Principle**: *"Consequences of environmental degradation do not respect national or state boundaries."* Ecological sustainability requires global cooperation and immediate shifts toward renewable energy sources.

## Part 5: Practice Bank & Original Predicted Questions

### Short Answer Practice Questions (2 Marks)

#### Question 1
**Q**: "Different people have different development goals." Explain this statement with two suitable examples.  
**Model Answer**:  
Different individuals have varying life circumstances, economic backgrounds, and needs, leading to distinct development goals.  
* **Example 1**: A landless rural labourer's goal is more days of work and better wages, whereas a prosperous farmer's goal is higher crop support prices and cheap labor.  
* **Example 2**: An urban unemployed youth seeks a stable job, whereas a girl from an urban rich family seeks equal freedom and independence to pursue studies abroad.

---

#### Question 2
**Q**: Why is Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) lower in Kerala compared to Haryana, despite Haryana having a higher per capita income?  
**Model Answer**:  
Kerala has a lower Infant Mortality Rate (6 per 1,000 live births) than Haryana (28) because Kerala has prioritized **public facilities and social infrastructure**. Kerala possesses adequate provisions of basic primary health care centers, medical facilities, a well-functioning Public Distribution System (PDS), and high female literacy rates, ensuring proper maternal and child care.

---

### Original 2027 Predicted Essay/Long Question (3 Marks)

#### Question 3
**Q**: "Per capita income is not an adequate indicator of human development." Evaluate this statement by comparing the criteria used by the World Bank and the UNDP.  
**Model Answer**:  
**Evaluation**:  
Per capita income measures only the average monetary income of a country's population, ignoring how that income is distributed or used for human well-being. Therefore, it is inadequate on its own.

1. **World Bank Criterion**:
   * Uses **Per Capita Income** in US Dollars (PPP) as the sole metric for classifying countries into rich, low-middle, and low-income categories.
   * **Limitation**: It ignores disparities in wealth distribution, healthcare access, education levels, and general quality of life.

2. **UNDP Criterion (Human Development Index - HDI)**:
   * Uses a comprehensive multi-dimensional approach combining three pillars:
     * **Health**: Measured by Life Expectancy at Birth.
     * **Education**: Measured by Mean Years of Schooling.
     * **Standard of Living**: Measured by Per Capita Gross National Income (GNI).
   * *Example*: Sri Lanka has a lower total economic size than India, yet ranks much higher (89 vs 130) on the HDI due to superior healthcare and education facilities.

**Conclusion**: The UNDP criterion provides a more realistic and holistic assessment of human progress than the World Bank's income-only approach.

## Part 6: 15-Question Multiple Choice Quiz (MCQ)

1. Development goals of different sections of society are:  
   (A) Always identical  
   (B) Often conflicting and varied  
   (C) Determined solely by the World Bank  
   (D) Unimportant for economic progress  
   **Answer**: **(A)** [Incorrect] / **(B) Often conflicting and varied**  
   *Rationale*: People's varying situations lead to different and sometimes conflicting goals (e.g., dam building for industrialists vs. displacement for tribals).

2. Per Capita Income is calculated by dividing total national income by:  
   (A) Total working population  
   (B) Total land area  
   (C) Total population of the country  
   (D) Total number of households  
   **Answer**: **(C) Total population of the country**  
   *Rationale*: Per Capita Income = Total Income ÷ Total Population.

3. Which organisation publishes the World Development Report?  
   (A) UNDP  
   (B) World Bank  
   (C) IMF  
   (D) UNESCO  
   **Answer**: **(B) World Bank**  
   *Rationale*: The World Bank uses Per Capita Income in its World Development Reports to classify countries.

4. India falls under which income group according to World Bank classification?  
   (A) High-income countries  
   (B) Low-income countries  
   (C) Low middle-income countries  
   (D) Upper middle-income countries  
   **Answer**: **(C) Low middle-income countries**  
   *Rationale*: India's per capita income (around US\$ 9,047 PPP) places it in the low middle-income tier.

5. Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) indicates the number of children dying before completing:  
   (A) 6 months of age per 100 live births  
   (B) 1 year of age per 1,000 live births  
   (C) 5 years of age per 100 live births  
   (D) 10 years of age per 1,000 live births  
   **Answer**: **(B) 1 year of age per 1,000 live births**  
   *Rationale*: Official definition of IMR is child deaths under 1 year per 1,000 live births.

6. Which Indian state among Haryana, Kerala, and Bihar has the highest literacy rate?  
   (A) Haryana  
   (B) Bihar  
   (C) Kerala  
   (D) Rajasthan  
   **Answer**: **(C) Kerala**  
   *Rationale*: Kerala achieves a literacy rate of 94% (2017–18 data).

7. What metric is used to evaluate adult nutritional status?  
   (A) Infant Mortality Rate  
   (B) Net Attendance Ratio  
   (C) Body Mass Index (BMI)  
   (D) Life Expectancy  
   **Answer**: **(C) Body Mass Index (BMI)**  
   *Rationale*: BMI = Weight (kg) / Height (m²), assessing adult nourishment.

8. A person is considered undernourished if their BMI is below:  
   (A) 25.0 kg/m²  
   (B) 18.5 kg/m²  
   (C) 22.5 kg/m²  
   (D) 30.0 kg/m²  
   **Answer**: **(B) 18.5 kg/m²**  
   *Rationale*: An adult BMI < 18.5 kg/m² denotes undernourishment.

9. Which organization publishes the Human Development Report (HDR)?  
   (A) World Bank  
   (B) UNDP  
   (C) WHO  
   (D) UNICEF  
   **Answer**: **(B) UNDP**  
   *Rationale*: UNDP (United Nations Development Programme) issues the annual HDR.

10. Which of the following is NOT a pillar of the Human Development Index (HDI)?  
    (A) Life Expectancy at Birth  
    (B) Per Capita Income  
    (C) Mean Years of Schooling  
    (D) Total Military Expenditure  
    **Answer**: **(D) Total Military Expenditure**  
    *Rationale*: HDI evaluates health, education, and per capita income.

11. Sri Lanka's HDI rank is superior to India's primarily because of its:  
    (A) Larger geographic size  
    (B) Better healthcare, education, and life expectancy  
    (C) Higher industrial steel production  
    (D) Huge military force  
    **Answer**: **(B) Better healthcare, education, and life expectancy**  
    *Rationale*: Sri Lanka excels in health (Life Expectancy 77.5 yrs) and education.

12. Which of the following is a renewable natural resource?  
    (A) Crude oil  
    (B) Groundwater  
    (C) Coal  
    (D) Natural Gas  
    **Answer**: **(B) Groundwater**  
    *Rationale*: Groundwater is naturally replenished by rainwater.

13. Overuse of groundwater in India is particularly severe in:  
    (A) Dense Himalayan forests  
    (B) Punjab and Western Uttar Pradesh  
    (C) Thar Desert sand dunes  
    (D) Andaman and Nicobar Islands  
    **Answer**: **(B) Punjab and Western Uttar Pradesh**  
    *Rationale*: Intensive agricultural tubewell irrigation in Punjab/Western UP causes severe depletion.

14. World crude oil reserves are estimated to last approximately how many years at current consumption rates?  
    (A) 500 years  
    (B) 50 years  
    (C) 200 years  
    (D) Indefinitely  
    **Answer**: **(B) 50 years**  
    *Rationale*: Energy Institute data projects existing reserves will last ~50 years.

15. "Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising future generations" is termed:  
    (A) Rapid Industrialisation  
    (B) Sustainable Development  
    (C) Absolute Capitalism  
    (D) Economic Inflation  
    **Answer**: **(B) Sustainable Development**  
    *Rationale*: Definition of sustainable development emphasizing inter-generational equity.
