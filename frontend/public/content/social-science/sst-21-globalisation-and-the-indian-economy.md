## 2. Complete Syllabus-Aligned Revision Notes

### 2.1 Production Across Countries & Multinational Corporations (MNCs)

* **Trade in the Mid-20th Century**: Until the mid-20th century, production was largely organized within national boundaries. Trade between countries consisted primarily of raw materials, food-stuffs, and finished consumer goods. Foreign trade connected distant countries through merchant channels.
* **Definition of a Multinational Corporation (MNC)**: A Multinational Corporation is a company that owns or controls production in more than one nation. MNCs set up offices and factories for production in regions where they can get cheap labour, raw materials, and other resources.
* **Global Dispersal of Production**:
  * MNCs do not produce the entire product in a single country; they break down the production process into small parts and spread them across the globe.
  * **Factors Determining Production Locations**:
    1. *Proximity to Markets*: Factories are set up close to major consumer markets (e.g., Mexico and Eastern Europe for US and European markets).
    2. *Availability of Cheap Labour*: Manufacturing is located where skilled and unskilled labour is available at low costs (e.g., China as a major cheap manufacturing hub).
    3. *Technical & Service Expertise*: Countries with large pools of educated, English-speaking youth provide technical and customer care services (e.g., India providing high-tech software engineers and call centres).
    4. *Government Policies*: Favourable tax laws, industrial infrastructure, and relaxed investment rules.
* **Cost Advantage**: Dispersing production globally allows MNCs to cut production costs by **50% to 60%**, giving them immense competitive strength in world markets.
* **NCERT Production Case Study**: An American industrial equipment MNC designs its products in research centres in the United States, manufactures the components in China (cheap manufacturing), ships them to Mexico and Eastern Europe for assembly, sells the finished products worldwide, and handles customer care out of call centres in Bengaluru, India.

### 2.2 Interlinking Production Across Countries

* **Investment vs. Foreign Investment**:
  * **Investment**: Money that is spent to buy assets such as land, buildings, machines, and other equipment in order to earn profits.
  * **Foreign Investment**: Investment made by Multinational Corporations (MNCs) in countries other than their home nation.
* **Four Major Routes through which MNCs Interlink Production Globally**:
  1. **Joint Ventures / Partnerships with Local Companies**:
     * MNCs enter foreign markets by partnering with existing local companies.
     * *Twofold Benefits for Local Companies*: MNCs provide additional capital for buying modern machinery and bring the latest international technology into production processes.
     * *Example*: In 1995, **Ford Motors** (US MNC) entered India by setting up a joint venture with **Mahindra & Mahindra** (Indian automobile maker) near Chennai, investing ₹1,700 crore to manufacture cars for both Indian and global markets.
  2. **Buying Up Local Companies (Direct Takeover)**:
     * The most common route for MNC expansion. MNCs possess vast wealth exceeding the entire national budgets of many developing countries, allowing them to easily buy up established domestic firms.
     * *Example*: **Cargill Foods** (a giant American MNC) bought **Parakh Foods** (a large Indian edible oil company). Parakh Foods had a vast marketing network across India and four oil refineries. Cargill took over its operations, becoming the largest producer of edible oil in India with a capacity of 5 million pouches daily.
  3. **Placing Orders with Small Producers (Sub-Contracting / Outsourcing)**:
     * MNCs place large orders for finished goods (garments, footwear, sports equipment, toys) with thousands of small, independent manufacturers across developing countries.
     * The MNCs receive the products, print their own brand names on them, and sell them to consumers worldwide at high prices.
     * *Power of MNCs*: MNCs determine the price, quality, delivery schedule, and labour working conditions for these distant small suppliers.
  4. **Technology Transfers and Licensing Agreements**:
     * Supplying proprietary designs, patents, and manufacturing processes to foreign producers under strict quality control agreements.

### 2.3 Foreign Trade and Market Integration

* **Primary Function of Foreign Trade**: Foreign trade creates an opportunity for domestic producers to reach markets beyond their national boundaries. Simultaneously, it expands consumer choices beyond domestically produced goods.
* **Market Integration Process**:
  * With the opening of trade, goods travel from one country to another.
  * Choice of goods in the markets rises significantly.
  * Prices of similar goods in two distant markets tend to equalize due to price competition.
  * Domestic and foreign producers compete directly with each other, integrating national markets into a single global market.
* **Textbook Case Study: *Chinese Toys in India***:
  * Chinese manufacturers began exporting plastic toys to India due to lower manufacturing costs.
  * Indian buyers got a wide variety of toys at significantly lower prices compared to traditional Indian wooden or clay toys.
  * Within a single year, Chinese toys replaced Indian toys in **70% to 80%** of toy shops.
  * *Economic Result*: Indian consumers benefitted from cheaper choices, but domestic Indian toy manufacturers faced severe business losses, forcing many small toy factories to shut down.

### 2.4 Meaning of Globalisation

* **Formal Definition**: Globalisation is the process of rapid integration or interconnectedness between countries through foreign trade and foreign investment, facilitated by Multinational Corporations (MNCs).
* **Four Major Dimensions / Global Flows**:
  1. *Flow of Goods and Services*: Movement of agricultural, manufactured, and service products across borders.
  2. *Flow of Capital / Investment*: Movement of money and foreign direct investments across nations.
  3. *Flow of Technology*: Rapid spread of ideas, software, designs, and manufacturing techniques.
  4. *Flow of People (Migration)*: Movement of people between countries in search of better employment, higher income, or education.

---

### 2.5 Factors that Have Enabled Globalisation (Enabler 1: Technology)

1. **Rapid Advances in Transportation Technology**:
   * Over the past 50 years, faster locomotives, long-haul cargo planes, and supertankers have made long-distance delivery of goods significantly faster and cheaper.
   * **Containerization**: Goods are packed into standardized steel containers that can be loaded intact onto ships, trains, trucks, and planes. Container handling has dramatically reduced port handling costs, minimized transit damage, and accelerated market delivery worldwide.
2. **Information and Communication Technology (IT & Telecom)**:
   * **Telecommunications**: Telegraph, mobile phones, fax, and satellite communication devices allow instant contact across the globe, remote decision-making, and real-time trade monitoring.
   * **Internet & Computers**: E-mail, World Wide Web, e-commerce, and cloud computing allow administrative, design, financial, and publishing services to be conducted remotely.
   * *Example of IT-enabled Outsourcing*: A news magazine published for readers in London is designed and printed in Delhi. The text is sent via the Internet to Delhi; editors in Delhi design the layout on computers using instructions received from London; the magazine is printed in Delhi and dispatched by air to London; payments are transferred instantly from London to Delhi via e-banking.

---

### 2.6 Factors that Have Enabled Globalisation (Enabler 2: Liberalisation of Foreign Trade & Investment)

* **Trade Barriers**:
  * A trade barrier is a government restriction or regulation imposed on international trade, such as import duties (taxes), quotas, or licensing requirements.
  * *Why Tax on Imports acts as a Barrier*: Importing goods attracts tax, making imported items more expensive in domestic markets, thereby protecting domestic producers from foreign price competition.
* **India's Trade Barrier Policy Post-Independence (1947–1990)**:
  * After independence in 1947, the Indian government put barriers on foreign trade and foreign investment to protect young domestic industries from competition by powerful foreign companies.
  * During the 1950s and 1960s, Indian industries were just coming up, and competition from imports would have killed these infant sectors. India allowed imports of only essential goods such as machinery, fertilizers, and petroleum.
* **New Economic Policy of 1991 & Liberalisation**:
  * Around 1991, the Indian government realized that Indian producers had matured and needed to compete with international producers to improve product quality, efficiency, and technology.
  * **Definition of Liberalisation**: Removing barriers or restrictions set by the government on foreign trade and foreign investment.
  * Under liberalisation, import licenses were abolished for almost all items, tariffs were drastically reduced, and foreign companies were allowed to invest freely in India.

### 2.7 World Trade Organization (WTO)

* **Establishment & Headquarters**: Established on **1 January 1995** (succeeding the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade - GATT). Headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland.
* **Core Objectives**:
  1. To liberalise international trade by removing trade barriers globally.
  2. To establish fair rules for international trade among member nations.
  3. To ensure that member countries strictly adhere to trade agreements and non-discriminatory policies.
* **Critical Issues & Debates Surrounding WTO**:
  * Although WTO aims to allow free trade for all, in practice, developed nations have unfairly retained trade barriers and massive subsidies (especially in agriculture), while forcing developing nations to remove their protective tariffs and agricultural subsidies.
  * *Example*: US farmers receive huge government subsidies, allowing them to dump artificially cheap cotton and wheat in international markets, devastating un-subsidized small farmers in developing countries like India.

---

### 2.8 Impact of Globalisation in India

* **Positive Impacts**:
  1. *Benefits to Consumers*: Urban consumers enjoy a wide variety of high-quality goods (automobiles, cell phones, electronics, fast food) at lower prices, leading to higher standards of living.
  2. *MNC Investments & Job Creation*: MNCs have invested heavily in Indian industries like cell phones, automobiles, electronics, banking, and services, creating direct employment and boosting local ancillary suppliers.
  3. *Emergence of Indian MNCs*: Top Indian companies leveraged competition to expand globally into multinationals—**Tata Motors** (automobiles), **Infosys** (IT services), **Ranbaxy** (pharmaceuticals), **Asian Paints** (paints), and **Sundaram Fasteners** (nuts and bolts).
  4. *Boom in IT & Service Outsourcing*: Millions of jobs created in IT, BPOs, call centres, financial accounting, software development, and administrative services.
* **Negative Impacts & Structural Challenges**:
  1. *Severe Blow to Small Producers*: Small-scale industries manufacturing batteries, capacitors, plastics, toys, tyres, and vegetable oil faced fierce import competition. Many small units shut down, rendering lakhs of workers unemployed.
  2. *Insecurity of Employment & Flexibilisation of Labour*: To cut costs, employers hire workers on a flexible, temporary, seasonal basis without provident fund, medical benefits, or job security (e.g., garment exporters in Surat, Tirupur, and Noida).
  3. *Widening Disparities*: Benefits of globalisation are concentrated among educated, skilled, and wealthy urban citizens, while unorganized workers, small producers, and landless farmers suffer increased economic vulnerability.

---

### 2.9 The Struggle for a Fair Globalisation

* **Concept of Fair Globalisation**: Fair globalisation creates equitable opportunities for all citizens and ensures that the benefits of global economic integration are shared fairly among all social groups, rather than benefitting only the rich and powerful.
* **How the Government Can Ensure Fair Globalisation**:
  1. **Enforce Labour Laws Strictly**: Ensure that workers' legal rights are protected and temporary/informal workers receive fair wages and social security.
  2. **Support Small Producers**: Provide credit, modern technology, and infrastructure to small-scale industries until they become strong enough to compete with MNCs.
  3. **Use Trade and Investment Barriers Strategically**: Retain targeted tariffs or import quotas when necessary to protect vulnerable domestic employment sectors.
  4. **Negotiate at the WTO for Fairer Rules**: Align with other developing nations to challenge agricultural subsidies and protectionist policies maintained by developed countries.
  5. **People's Campaign Influence**: Mass campaigns, NGOs, and trade union representations can shape international trade negotiations and force government policy revisions.

### 3.2 Foreign Investment vs. Domestic Investment

| Feature | Foreign Investment (FDI) | Domestic Investment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Investment made by foreign entities / MNCs in a host country. | Investment made by domestic citizens, companies, or government within their nation. |
| **Primary Source** | Foreign capital reserves, foreign banks, or global capital markets. | Domestic household savings, domestic commercial banks, and national financial institutions. |
| **Main Objective** | To expand international markets, cut production costs, and maximize profits. | To build domestic industrial capacity, generate regional employment, and earn local profit. |
| **Technology Linkage**| Brings modern international technology and advanced global management practices. | Relies on existing domestic technology or imported technology licenses. |
| **Example** | Cargill Foods purchasing Parakh Foods or Ford Motors setting up a factory near Chennai. | An Indian entrepreneur setting up a local textile mill using domestic bank loans. |

---

### 3.3 Trade Barriers vs. Liberalisation

| Criterion | Trade Barriers | Liberalisation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Government restrictions/taxes imposed on foreign trade and investment. | Removal or relaxation of government restrictions on foreign trade and investment. |
| **Tools / Instruments**| Import duties (tariffs), import quotas, strict licensing rules. | Abolition of import licenses, reduction of customs tariffs, free currency movement. |
| **Primary Objective**| To protect infant domestic industries from competition by powerful foreign firms. | To increase competition, improve quality, adopt modern tech, and integrate with global markets. |
| **Impact on Prices** | Makes imported goods expensive, protecting domestic prices. | Lowers prices of imported goods, increasing market choices for consumers. |
| **Policy Period in India**| Prominently followed from 1947 to 1990. | Adopted under the New Economic Policy from 1991 onwards. |

---

### 3.4 Positive vs. Negative Impacts of Globalisation on the Indian Economy

| Aspect | Positive Impacts | Negative Impacts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Consumer Experience** | Wider choice, superior international quality, lower prices for urban consumers. | Traditional local goods are displaced; small consumers lose access to cheap artisanal products. |
| **Industrial Growth** | MNC investment in automobiles, cell phones, electronics, and fast food sectors. | Small-scale manufacturers (batteries, toys, tyres, capacitors) suffer heavy losses or shutdown. |
| **Employment Trends** | Creation of high-paying jobs in IT, BPO, software, and skilled service sectors. | Informalisation of industrial labour; workers hired on flexible, temporary terms without security. |
| **Domestic Companies** | Indian companies transform into global MNCs (Tata Motors, Infosys, Ranbaxy). | Uncompetitive domestic small units are forced out of business due to cheap foreign imports. |
| **Economic Disparity** | Rapid income growth for educated, skilled, and wealthy urban population. | Widening gap between prosperous urban beneficiaries and insecure rural/unorganized workers. |

### Original Predicted Board Exam Questions (2027 Examination)

#### Predicted Essay Question 1 (04 Marks - Essay / Long Answer)
**"Globalisation has produced mixed results for the Indian economy." Critically evaluate this statement by discussing two positive and two negative impacts of globalisation on India.**

**Official BSER Marking Scheme (04 Marks Total)**:
* *Definition / Contextual Introduction* (01 Mark)
* *Two Positive Impacts explained with examples* (1.5 Marks)
* *Two Negative Impacts explained with examples* (1.5 Marks)

**Model Answer**:  
**Introduction**: Globalisation—the rapid integration of national economies through foreign trade and investment—has brought profound transformations to the Indian economy since the 1991 liberalisation reforms, producing both significant gains and structural challenges.

**Positive Impacts**:
1. *Consumer Choice & Higher Living Standards*: Urban consumers enjoy access to superior quality, technologically advanced products (automobiles, mobile phones, electronics, fast food) at competitive prices.
2. *Investment & Global Indian MNCs*: Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) created millions of service and manufacturing jobs. Furthermore, top Indian firms expanded into multinationals globally (e.g., Tata Motors, Infosys, Ranbaxy, Asian Paints).

**Negative Impacts**:
1. *Destruction of Small-Scale Industries*: Small domestic producers of batteries, capacitors, plastics, toys, tyres, and vegetable oil could not compete with cheap foreign imports, leading to widespread unit closures and job retrenchments.
2. *Flexibilisation and Insecurity of Labour*: To cut costs, export industries hire workers on a temporary, informal basis without social security, medical benefits, or job permanence (e.g., garment units in Surat and Noida).

**Conclusion**: While globalisation modernized India's urban service and corporate sectors, it harmed small producers and unorganized workers, highlighting the need for government policy intervention.

#### Predicted Essay Question 2 (04 Marks - Essay / Long Answer with Choice)
**What is meant by 'Fair Globalisation'? Suggest three concrete measures that the government can adopt to make globalisation fairer in India.**

**Official BSER Marking Scheme (04 Marks Total)**:
* *Concept of Fair Globalisation* (01 Mark)
* *Three Government Measures clearly explained* (03 Marks - 01 Mark each)

**Model Answer**:  
**Concept of Fair Globalisation**: Fair globalisation refers to a system of global economic integration that creates equitable opportunities for all citizens and ensures that the benefits of international trade and investment are shared fairly among all social classes, rather than benefitting only the rich, educated, and powerful.

**Three Government Measures for Fair Globalisation**:
1. **Strict Enforcement of Labour Laws**: The government must ensure that worker rights are protected under law, enforcing proper wages, safe working conditions, social security, and preventing arbitrary retrenchment of temporary workers.
2. **Support & Protection for Small Producers**: The government must provide cheap credit, modern infrastructure, technology upgrades, and marketing support to small-scale industries until they become strong enough to compete with giant MNCs.
3. **Strategic Use of Trade Barriers & WTO Diplomacy**: The government should retain targeted import duties or tariffs on vulnerable agricultural and small industrial products. At the international level, India must align with other developing nations to negotiate at the WTO against unfair agricultural subsidies maintained by developed countries.

*Rationale*: Flexibilisation of labour laws allows companies to hire temporary workers to cut labour costs.

#### Q14. What is the primary role of the World Trade Organization (WTO)?
(A) To provide interest-free loans to poor countries  
(B) To liberalise international trade and enforce trade rules  
(C) To regulate international currency exchange rates  
(D) To manage environmental conservation globally  
**Answer**: **(B)**  
*Rationale*: WTO's main role is to facilitate free international trade and administer multilateral trade rules.

#### Q15. Which of the following measures can ensure 'Fair Globalisation' in India?
(A) Total removal of all labour welfare laws  
(B) Strict enforcement of labour laws and support for small producers  
(C) Banning all foreign investment completely  
(D) Closing domestic markets to international technology  
**Answer**: **(B)**  
*Rationale*: Protecting worker rights under labour laws and assisting small producers ensures fair sharing of globalisation benefits.
