### Theme 1: Meaning and Classification of Resources

#### A. Definition of Resource
Everything available in our environment that can be used to satisfy human needs, provided it is **technologically accessible**, **economically feasible**, and **culturally acceptable**, is termed a **Resource**.
* **Interdependence Process**: Human beings interact with nature through **technology** and create **institutions** to accelerate their economic development.

#### B. Comprehensive Classification of Resources

```
                            RESOURCES
                                |
        +-----------------------+-----------------------+
        |                                               |
     NATURAL                                          HUMAN
        |                                               |
  +-----+-----+                                   +-----+-----+
  |           |                                   |           |
RENEWABLE  NON-RENEWABLE                     STRUCTURES &   QUANTITY &
  |           |                              INSTITUTIONS    QUALITY
  +-- Continuous/Flow (Wind, Water)               |
  +-- Biological (Flora & Fauna)                  +-- Recyclable (Metals)
                                                  +-- Non-Recyclable (Fossil Fuels)
```

1. **On the Basis of Origin**:
   * **Biotic Resources**: Obtained from the biosphere and have life. *Examples*: Human beings, flora, fauna, fisheries, livestock.
   * **Abiotic Resources**: Composed of non-living things. *Examples*: Rocks, minerals, metals, land.

2. **On the Basis of Exhaustibility**:
   * **Renewable Resources**: Can be renewed or reproduced by physical, chemical, or mechanical processes. *Examples*: Solar energy, wind energy, water, forests, wildlife.
   * **Non-Renewable Resources**: Occur over a very long geological time (millions of years). They get exhausted with use. *Examples*: Minerals, fossil fuels (coal, petroleum). Metallic minerals are recyclable, whereas fossil fuels are non-recyclable.

3. **On the Basis of Ownership**:
   * **Individual Resources**: Owned privately by individuals. *Examples*: Urban plots, houses, agricultural land owned by farmers, plantation plots, pasture lands, ponds.
   * **Community Owned Resources**: Accessible to all members of the community. *Examples*: Village commons (grazing grounds, burial grounds, village ponds), public parks, picnic spots, playgrounds in urban areas.
   * **National Resources**: Technically, all resources within the political boundaries and territorial waters of a nation belong to the nation. The government has legal powers to acquire private property for public good.
     * *Territorial Water Limit*: Oceanic area up to **12 nautical miles** (22.2 km) from the coast belongs to the nation.
   * **International Resources**: Regulated by international institutions.
     * *Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)*: Oceanic resources beyond **200 nautical miles** of the EEZ belong to open ocean; no individual country can utilize them without the concurrence of international institutions.

4. **On the Basis of the Status of Development**:
   * **Potential Resources**: Found in a region but have not been utilized. *Example*: Solar and wind energy potential in Rajasthan and Gujarat that is not yet fully developed.
   * **Developed Resources**: Surveyed and their quality and quantity determined for utilization. The development of resources depends on technology and level of their feasibility.
   * **Stock**: Materials in the environment that have the potential to satisfy human needs but human beings do not have the appropriate technology to access them. *Example*: Water ($H_2O$) is a compound of hydrogen and oxygen, which are rich sources of energy, but we lack technical 'know-how' to use them for energy on a large scale.
   * **Reserves**: Subset of the stock, which can be put into use with the help of existing technical 'know-how', but their use has not been started. They are kept for meeting future requirements. *Example*: River water used for generating hydroelectric power in a limited quantity, water in dams, forests.

### Theme 2: Development of Resources & Sustainable Development

#### A. Major Problems Caused by Indiscriminate Exploitation of Resources
Human beings used resources indiscriminately under the assumption that they are free gifts of nature. This led to major crises:
1. **Depletion of resources** for satisfying the greed of a few individuals.
2. **Accumulation of resources** in a few hands, dividing society into two segments: 'haves' and 'have-nots' (rich and poor).
3. **Global Ecological Crises**: Global warming, ozone layer depletion, environmental pollution, and land degradation.

#### B. Sustainable Development
* **Definition**: Sustainable economic development means **"development should take place without damaging the environment, and development in the present should not compromise the needs of future generations."**

#### C. Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit, 1992
* **Date & Place**: June 1992, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil (First International Earth Summit).
* **Participants**: Heads of more than 100 states.
* **Objective**: Address urgent problems of environmental protection and socio-economic development at the global level.
* **Outcome**: Declaration on Global Climatic Change and Biological Diversity; endorsed the Global Forest Principles and adopted **Agenda 21**.

#### D. Agenda 21
* Declaration signed by world leaders at UNCED (United Nations Conference on Environment and Development) in 1992.
* **Aim**: Achieving global sustainable development.
* **Key Objective**: Combat environmental damage, poverty, and disease through global co-operation on common interests, mutual needs, and shared responsibilities.
* **Major Feature**: Every local government should draw its own local Agenda 21.

#### E. Gandhian Philosophy on Resource Conservation
* Mahatma Gandhi stated: **"There is enough for everybody's need and not for any body's greed."**
* He placed the greedy and selfish individuals and exploitative nature of modern technology as the root cause for resource depletion at the global level.
* He was against mass production and wanted to replace it with **production by the masses**.

### Theme 3: Resource Planning in India

#### A. Need for Resource Planning in India
Resource planning is essential in a country like India due to **enormous diversity and regional disparities** in resource availability:
1. **Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, and Madhya Pradesh**: Rich in minerals and coal deposits, but lack infrastructure.
2. **Arunachal Pradesh**: Abundant water resources, but lacks infrastructural development.
3. **Rajasthan**: Well endowed with solar and wind energy, but lacks water resources.
4. **Ladakh (Cold Desert)**: Isolated from the rest of the country; rich cultural heritage, but deficient in water, infrastructure, and vital minerals.

#### B. Three Stages of Resource Planning in India
Resource planning is a complex process involving three distinct steps:

```
Step 1: Identification & Inventory ──► Step 2: Planning Structure ──► Step 3: National Alignment
(Surveying, Mapping, Estimations)     (Technology, Skill, Institutions)   (Matching with Five Year Plans)
```

1. **Identification and Inventory of Resources**: Surveying, mapping, qualitative and quantitative estimation, and measurement of resources across all regions of the country.
2. **Evolving a Planning Structure**: Endowed with appropriate technology, skill, and institutional set-up for implementing resource development plans.
3. **Matching Resource Development Plans**: Aligning regional resource development plans with overall national development plans (Five Year Plans starting from the First Five Year Plan launched after Independence).

### Theme 4: Land Resources & Land Utilisation Pattern

#### A. Land Relief Features of India
India's total geographical area is **3.28 million sq km**.

| Landform Relief | Percentage Share | Economic Significance |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Plains** | **43%** | Provides facility for agriculture and industry. |
| **Mountains** | **30%** | Ensures perennial flow of rivers; provides facilities for tourism and ecological balance. |
| **Plateaus** | **27%** | Possesses rich reserves of minerals, fossil fuels, and forests. |

#### B. Land Utilisation Categories in India
Land resources are used for the following purposes:
1. **Forests**
2. **Land not available for cultivation**:
   * (a) Barren and waste land.
   * (b) Land put to non-agricultural uses (buildings, roads, factories, railways).
3. **Other uncultivated land (excluding fallow land)**:
   * (a) Permanent pastures and grazing land.
   * (b) Land under miscellaneous tree crops and groves (not included in net sown area).
   * (c) Culturable waste land (left uncultivated for more than 5 agricultural years).
4. **Fallow lands**:
   * (a) **Current fallow**: Left without cultivation for one or less than one agricultural year.
   * (b) **Other than current fallow**: Left uncultivated for the past 1 to 5 agricultural years.
5. **Net Sown Area (NSA)**: Physical area sown with crops at least once in an agricultural year.
   * **Gross Cropped Area**: Area sown more than once in an agricultural year plus Net Sown Area.

#### C. Land Use Pattern and Variations in India
* **Data Availability**: Land use data is available for only **93%** of the total geographical area (3.28 million sq km) because land use reporting for most of the North-East states (except Assam) has not been done fully, and some areas of Jammu & Kashmir occupied by Pakistan and China have not been surveyed.
* **National Forest Policy (1952) Benchmark**: Prescribed **33% of geographical area** under forest cover for maintaining ecological balance. India's actual forest area remains far below this desired target.
* **Regional Variations in Net Sown Area (NSA)**:
  * **High NSA**: Over **80%** of total area in **Punjab and Haryana**.
  * **Low NSA**: Less than **10%** in **Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Manipur, and Andaman & Nicobar Islands**.

### Theme 5: Land Degradation and Conservation Measures

#### A. Causes of Land Degradation
Continuous use of land over a long period without taking appropriate conservation measures has led to degradation. About **130 million hectares** of degraded land exists in India.

| Cause of Degradation | Affected States / Regions | Mechanism / Mechanism of Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Deforestation & Mining** | Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha | Mining sites left abandoned with deep scars and overburdening. |
| **Overgrazing** | Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra | Livestock grazing destroys grass cover and exposes topsoil. |
| **Over-irrigation** | Punjab, Haryana, Western Uttar Pradesh | Waterlogging leads to increase in salinity and alkalinity in soil. |
| **Mineral Processing** | Cement & Ceramic Industry hubs | Grinding of limestone produces huge dust clouds that settle on land and retard water infiltration. |

#### B. Land Conservation Measures
1. **Afforestation** and proper management of grazing lands.
2. Planting of **shelter belts** of plants.
3. **Stabilization of sand dunes** by growing thorny bushes in arid regions (e.g. Western Rajasthan).
4. Proper management of waste lands and control of mining activities.
5. Proper treatment and discharge of industrial effluents before disposal.

### Theme 6: Soil as a Resource & Major Soil Types of India

#### A. Factors of Soil Formation
Soil is the most important renewable natural resource. Factors influencing soil formation:
* **Parent rock / Bed rock**: Determines color, texture, chemical properties, and mineral content.
* **Climate**: Temperature and rainfall determine rate of weathering and humus formation.
* **Relief / Topography**: Altitude and slope determine accumulation of soil.
* **Vegetation & Life Forms**: Affect rate of humus formation.
* **Time**: Determines thickness of soil profile.

#### B. Comparative Matrix of India's Major Soil Types

| Soil Type | Formation & Parent Material | Key Characteristics & Texture | Chemical Composition & Nutrients | Major Geographical Distribution | Crops Grown |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Alluvial Soil** | Deposited by three major Himalayan river systems: Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra. | Very fertile; classified by age into **Bhangar** (old, kankar nodules, less fertile) and **Khadar** (new, fine particles, highly fertile). | Rich in **Potash, Phosphoric Acid, Lime**; deficient in Nitrogen and Organic Matter. | Northern Plains, coastal deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri. | Sugarcane, Paddy (Rice), Wheat, Pulses, Coarse Grains. |
| **Black Soil** *(Regur / Cotton Soil)* | Formed from basalt / lava flows (Deccan trap region). | Black in color; clayey material; high moisture retention; develops deep cracks in dry weather (**self-aeration**); sticky when wet. | Rich in **Calcium Carbonate, Magnesium, Potash, Lime**; poor in Phosphoric content. | Maharashtra, Saurashtra, Malwa, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Godavari-Krishna valleys. | **Cotton** (primary crop), Sugarcane, Wheat, Tobacco. |
| **Red & Yellow Soils** | Developed on crystalline igneous rocks in areas of low rainfall. | Reddish color due to **diffusion of iron** in crystalline and metamorphic rocks; looks yellow when hydrated. | Rich in Iron and Potash; deficient in Nitrogen, Humus, and Phosphoric acid. | Eastern & Southern Deccan plateau, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Piedmont zone of Western Ghats. | Groundnut, Pulses, Millets, Rice. |
| **Laterite Soil** | Derived from Latin *later* (brick). Formed in tropical/subtropical climate with alternate wet/dry seasons via intense leaching. | Highly acidic ($pH < 6.0$); deep to very deep; low humus content as microorganisms die in high temperatures. | Deficient in Nitrogen, Potash, Lime, and Organic matter. | Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Madhya Pradesh, hilly areas of Odisha and Assam. | **Cashew nut** (Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala), Tea, Coffee. |
| **Arid Soil** | Formed in arid & semi-arid conditions under high temperature and rapid evaporation. | Ranges from red to brown; sandy texture, saline nature; lacks moisture and humus. Lower horizons occupied by **Kankar** (calcium nodules). | High salt content (salt obtained by evaporating water); rich in soluble salts, poor in organic matter. | Western Rajasthan (Thar Desert region). | Bajra, Pulses, Coarse grains (cultivable with irrigation like Indira Gandhi Canal). |
| **Forest / Mountain Soil** | Formed in snow-covered and forested mountain areas with sufficient rainfall. | Heterogeneous texture: loamy and silty in valley sides, coarse-grained in upper slopes. Acidic with low humus in snow areas. | Low humus in snow-clad regions; fertile in lower valley terraces and alluvial fans. | Himalayan regions (Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh). | Tea, Coffee, Spices, Tropical & Temperate Fruits (Apple, Peach). |

### Theme 7: Soil Erosion and Conservation

#### A. Definition and Causes of Soil Erosion
* **Soil Erosion**: The denudation of the soil cover and subsequent washing down is described as soil erosion.
* **Balance**: The processes of soil formation and erosion go on simultaneously and generally there is a balance between the two. The balance is disturbed by human activities (deforestation, overgrazing, construction, mining) and natural forces (wind, glacier, water).

#### B. Types of Soil Erosion
1. **Water Erosion**:
   * **Sheet Erosion**: Water flows as a sheet over large areas down a slope, washing away topsoil.
   * **Gully Erosion**: Running water cuts through clayey soils and makes deep channels called gullies.
   * **Badlands / Ravines**: Land intersected by gullies becomes unfit for cultivation. In the Chambal basin, such lands are called **Chambal Ravines**.
2. **Wind Erosion**: Wind blows loose, dry soil off flat or sloping land.
3. **Defective Agricultural Practices**: Ploughing up and down the slope forms channels for quick flow of water leading to soil wash.

#### C. Soil Conservation Methods

```
                               SOIL CONSERVATION METHODS
                                           |
    +-------------------+------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
    |                   |                                      |                   |
Contour Ploughing   Terrace Farming                      Strip Cropping      Shelter Belts
(Ploughing along    (Steps cut on slopes                (Strips of grass     (Rows of trees
 contour lines)     in Western Himalayas)                between crops)      stabilize sand dunes)
```

1. **Contour Ploughing**: Ploughing along the contour lines decelerates the flow of water down the slopes.
2. **Terrace Farming**: Cutting steps on slopes to create terraces. Restricts erosion; practiced widely in Western and Central Himalayas.
3. **Strip Cropping**: Large fields divided into strips. Strips of grass are left to grow between crops to break the force of the wind.
4. **Shelter Belts**: Planting rows of trees to create shelter. Stabilize sand dunes and deserts in Western Rajasthan.

* **Model Answer**:
  1. Soil formation is a slow process where parent rock/bedrock undergoes weathering due to climatic factors like temperature, rainfall, and freezing. [1 Mark]

#### Question 6 [BSER 2023 Main Exam]
**Classify resources on the basis of exhaustibility with examples.** [3 Marks]

    (D) Over-irrigation
    Answer: (B)
    Rationale: Iron diffusing into crystalline and metamorphic rocks gives the soil its characteristic reddish hue.

[11] Which soil type develops deep cracks during hot dry weather, facilitating self-aeration?
    (A) Alluvial Soil
    (B) Arid Soil
    (C) Black Soil
    (D) Laterite Soil
    Answer: (C)
    Rationale: Black soil has high clay content, expanding when wet and shrinking to form deep cracks when dry, aiding aeration.

[12] Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) extends oceanic resource ownership of a country up to:
    (A) 12 nautical miles
    (B) 100 nautical miles
    (C) 200 nautical miles
    (D) 500 nautical miles
    Answer: (C)
    Rationale: Beyond 200 nautical miles of the EEZ, oceanic resources belong to international institutions.

[13] Laterite soil is highly suitable for growing which of the following cash crops with proper manure application?
    (A) Wheat and Rice
    (B) Cashew nut and Tea
    (C) Sugarcane and Jute
    (D) Pulses and Mustard
    Answer: (B)
    Rationale: Laterite soils in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Kerala are suitable for tree crops like cashew nut, tea, and coffee.

[14] Wind blowing loose soil off flat or sloping land is known as:
    (A) Sheet erosion
    (B) Gully erosion
    (C) Wind erosion
    (D) Glacial erosion
    Answer: (C)
    Rationale: Wind erosion occurs when strong winds blow away topsoil from dry, unvegetated flat or sloping terrains.
