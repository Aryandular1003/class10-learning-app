## Syllabus-Aligned Complete Revision Notes

### 1. Concept and Key Features of Federalism
* **Definition**: Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and various constituent units of the country.
* **Tiers of Government**: Generally, a federation has two or more levels (tiers) of government:
  1. **Central/Union Government**: Responsible for the entire country and matters of national interest.
  2. **State/Provincial Government**: Looks after the day-to-day administration of individual states.
  3. **Local Self-Government** (in India): Third tier added via the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments (1992).
* **Key Features of Federalism (7 Principles)**:
  1. **Multiple Tiers**: There are two or more levels of government.
  2. **Specific Jurisdiction**: Different tiers of government govern the same citizens, but each tier has its own *jurisdiction* (legal authority) in specific matters of legislation, taxation, and administration.
  3. **Constitutional Guarantee**: The jurisdictions of the respective levels or tiers of government are specified in the Constitution, granting constitutional status and protection.
  4. **Rigid Constitutional Modification**: The fundamental provisions of the Constitution cannot be unilaterally changed by one level of government. Such changes require the consent of both levels of government.
  5. **Judicial Umpiring**: Courts (especially the Supreme Court) have the power to interpret the Constitution and the powers of different levels of government. The highest court acts as an umpire if disputes arise between different levels of government in the exercise of their respective powers.
  6. **Financial Autonomy**: Sources of revenue for each level of government are clearly specified to ensure its financial independence.
  7. **Dual Objectives**: The federal system has dual objectives:
     * To safeguard and promote the **unity** of the country.
     * To accommodate **regional diversity**.

### 2. Routes of Federation Formation

| Feature / Criteria | "Coming Together" Federations | "Holding Together" Federations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin & Process** | Independent States voluntarily come together on their own to form a bigger unit to pool sovereignty and retain identity. | A large country decides to divide its power between the constituent states and the national government. |
| **Objective** | To increase security and efficiency by pooling resources while preserving state sovereignty. | To accommodate regional, cultural, and linguistic diversity within a single nation. |
| **Power Distribution** | All constituent States usually have equal power and are strong vis-à-vis the central government. | The Central Government tends to be more powerful vis-à-vis the States; constituent units often have unequal powers. |
| **Examples** | **USA**, **Switzerland**, **Australia**. | **India**, **Spain**, **Belgium**. |

### 3. What Makes India a Federal Country? (Constitutional Division of Powers)

The Indian Constitution explicitly provides a **three-fold distribution of legislative powers** between the Union Government and the State Governments across three main lists:

1. **Union List**:
   * Contains subjects of **national importance** requiring a uniform policy throughout the country.
   * **Key Subjects**: Defence, Foreign Affairs, Banking, Communications, Currency, Atomic Energy.
   * **Legislative Power**: The **Union Parliament alone** can make laws on these subjects.
2. **State List**:
   * Contains subjects of **state and local importance**.
   * **Key Subjects**: Police, Trade, Commerce, Agriculture, Irrigation, Public Health.
   * **Legislative Power**: The **State Legislative Assembly alone** can make laws on these subjects under normal circumstances.
3. **Concurrent List**:
   * Contains subjects of **common interest** to both the Union Government and State Governments.
   * **Key Subjects**: Education, Forests, Trade Unions, Marriage, Adoption, Succession.
   * **Legislative Power**: Both Parliament and State Assemblies can make laws. **Conflict Rule**: If their laws conflict with each other, the law made by the **Union Parliament prevails**.
4. **Residuary Subjects**:
   * Subjects that do not fall in any of the three lists, or subjects that came up after the Constitution was drafted (e.g., Computer Software, Information Technology, Cyber Law).
   * **Legislative Power**: According to the Constitution, the **Union Government** has the sole power to legislate on residuary subjects.

#### Special Constitutional Status & Union Territories
* **Unequal Powers Among States**: Not all states in India have identical powers. Some states enjoy special status under Article 371 (e.g., Nagaland, Mizoram, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh) due to social/historical circumstances, protecting indigenous cultures and land rights.
* **Union Territories (UTs)**: These are areas that are too small to become an independent State but could not be merged with any existing State (e.g., Chandigarh, Lakshadweep, Delhi). These territories do not have the powers of a State and are administered directly by the **Central Government**.
* **Amendment Procedure**: The basic structure of power sharing cannot be easily altered. Any change must first be passed by both Houses of Parliament with at least a **two-thirds majority**, and then ratified by the legislatures of **at least half of the total States**.

### 4. How is Federalism Practised in India?

Federalism in India has succeeded not merely due to constitutional provisions, but primarily because of the democratic politics of the country.

1. **Linguistic States**:
   * The creation of Linguistic States was the first and a major test for democratic politics in India.
   * In 1956 (States Reorganisation Act), borders of several old states were changed to ensure that people who spoke the same language lived in the same State (e.g., Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu).
   * Some states were created not on language basis but to recognize differences based on culture, ethnicity, or geography (e.g., Nagaland, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand).
   * **Outcome**: Experience has shown that creating linguistic states actually made the country more united and administration easier.
2. **Language Policy**:
   * A second test for Indian federation was the language policy.
   * **No National Language**: The Constitution did not give the status of national language to any one language. Hindi was identified as the official language (spoken by ~40% of Indians).
   * **22 Scheduled Languages**: Besides Hindi, 21 other languages are recognised as Scheduled Languages by the Constitution in the **Eighth Schedule**. A candidate in an all-India central government examination may opt to take the examination in any of these languages.
   * **Official Use of English**: According to the Constitution, the use of English for official purposes was to stop in 1965. However, non-Hindi speaking states (especially Tamil Nadu) demanded that the use of English continue. The Central Government responded by agreeing to continue the use of English along with Hindi for official purposes.
3. **Centre-State Relations**:
   * **Pre-1990 Era**: For a long time, the same party ruled both at the Centre and in most States. When rival parties ruled at the State level, the Central Government often misused Article 356 to dismiss State Governments arbitrarily.
   * **Post-1990 Era (Coalition Era)**: After 1990, regional political parties emerged across many states. Since no single national party secured a clear majority in the Lok Sabha, major national parties had to form **Coalition Governments** with regional parties.
   * **Impact**: This led to a new culture of power sharing, respect for State autonomy, and was backed by a landmark Supreme Court judgment making it difficult for the Centre to dismiss state governments arbitrarily.

### 5. Decentralisation in India (3rd Tier of Governance)

* **Definition**: When power is taken away from Central and State governments and given to local government, it is called **Decentralisation**.
* **Rationale**:
  * Large number of problems and issues are best settled at the local level.
  * Local people have better knowledge of problems in their localities, where to spend money, and how to manage things efficiently.
  * Facilitates direct participation of citizens in decision-making, instilling democratic habits.

#### The 1992 Constitutional Amendment (73rd & 74th Amendments)
Before 1992, local bodies were under the direct control of state governments, elections were irregular, and local bodies lacked resources. The 1992 Amendment made decentralisation effective through **5 key constitutional mandates**:

1. **Mandatory Regular Elections**: It is constitutionally mandatory to hold regular elections to local government bodies.
2. **Reserved Seats for Disadvantaged Groups**: Seats are reserved in elected bodies and executive heads for Scheduled Castes (SCs), Scheduled Tribes (STs), and Other Backward Classes (OBCs).
3. **Women Representation**: At least **one-third (33%)** of all positions are reserved for women.
4. **State Election Commission (SEC)**: An independent body called the State Election Commission has been created in each State to conduct panchayat and municipal elections.
5. **State Finance Commission & Power Sharing**: State governments are required to share some powers and revenues with local government bodies.

### 6. Structure of Local Self-Government in India

```
                          LOCAL SELF-GOVERNMENT
                                    |
          +-------------------------+-------------------------+
          |                                                   |
  RURAL LOCAL GOVERNMENT                            URBAN LOCAL GOVERNMENT
  (Panchayati Raj System)                           (Urban Local Bodies)
          |                                                   |
  1. Gram Panchayat (Village Level)                 1. Municipalities (Towns)
     - Sarpanch & Panchs                               - Municipal Chairperson
     - Supervised by Gram Sabha                               |
          |                                         2. Municipal Corporations
  2. Panchayat Samiti / Block (Block Level)            (Large Cities)
     - Group of Gram Panchayats                        - Headed by Mayor
     - BDO & Samiti Chairperson                               |
          |                                                   |
  3. Zilla Parishad (District Level)                          |
     - All Samitis in a District                              |
     - Headed by Zilla Parishad Chairperson                   |
```

#### A. Rural Local Government (Panchayati Raj)
1. **Gram Panchayat**:
   * Decision-making body for a village or group of villages.
   * Consists of several ward members (*Panchs*) and a President (*Sarpanch*), directly elected by all adult citizens residing in that ward/village.
2. **Gram Sabha**:
   * The general body consisting of **all adult voters** in a village.
   * Meets at least 2 to 3 times a year to approve the annual budget of the Gram Panchayat and review its performance.
3. **Panchayat Samiti / Block / Mandal**:
   * Formed by grouping a few Gram Panchayats together at the block level.
   * Members are elected by all the panchayat members in that area.
4. **Zilla Parishad**:
   * The apex body at the district level, comprising all Panchayat Samitis in a district.
   * Members include elected representatives, local Members of Parliament (MPs), Members of Legislative Assembly (MLAs), and district officials.
   * The **Zilla Parishad Chairperson** is the political head of the Zilla Parishad.

#### B. Urban Local Government
1. **Municipalities**:
   * Set up in towns and smaller urban areas.
   * Headed by a **Municipal Chairperson** (political head).
2. **Municipal Corporations**:
   * Set up in big cities (e.g., Jaipur, Jodhpur, Kota, Delhi, Mumbai).
   * Headed by an elected political head called the **Mayor**.

## Technical Glossary

* **Jurisdiction**: The officially defined legal field over which someone has authority. The boundary may be defined in terms of geographical area or in terms of specific subjects.
* **Coalition Government**: A government formed together by an alliance of two or more political parties when no single party wins a clear majority in elections.
* **Decentralisation**: The transfer of power, decision-making, and resources from central/state governments to local self-governing bodies.
* **Residuary Powers**: Legislative powers over topics not enumerated in Union, State, or Concurrent lists, assigned exclusively to the Union Parliament.
* **Scheduled Languages**: The 22 languages listed in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution.

### 2. Short Answer Questions (2 & 3 Marks)

**Q5. Define 'Decentralisation'. Mention two main steps taken in the 1992 Constitutional Amendment to make decentralisation effective.**  
*(Source: BSER Secondary Examination 2024, Paper Code S-08, Weightage: 2 Marks)*  
* **Model Answer**:
  * **Definition**: When power is taken away from Central and State governments and given to local self-governments, it is called **Decentralisation**.
  * **Two Steps of 1992 Amendment**:
    1. **Mandatory Regular Elections**: It was made constitutionally mandatory to hold regular elections to local government bodies.
    2. **Women Reservation**: At least **one-third (33%)** of all positions in local bodies were reserved for women.

**Q6. Differentiate between 'Coming Together' Federations and 'Holding Together' Federations with one example of each.**  
*(Source: BSER Supplementary Examination 2024, Paper Code S-124, Weightage: 2 Marks)*  
* **Model Answer**:
  1. **Coming Together Federations**: Independent states voluntarily come together on their own to form a bigger unit, pooling sovereignty while retaining identity (e.g., **USA**, Switzerland).
  2. **Holding Together Federations**: A large country decides to divide its power between constituent states and the national government (e.g., **India**, Spain, Belgium).

**Q7. Explain any three key features of the federal system in India.**  
*(Source: BSER Secondary Examination 2023, Paper Code S-08, Weightage: 3 Marks)*  
* **Model Answer**:
  1. **Three-Tier System**: India has three levels of government—Union, State, and Local (Panchayats and Municipalities).
  2. **Three-Fold Distribution of Legislative Powers**: The Constitution explicitly divides legislative powers between Union and States into Union List, State List, and Concurrent List.
  3. **Judicial Supremacy**: The Supreme Court acts as an umpire to resolve constitutional disputes between the Union and State governments.

## Practice Bank & Original Predicted Questions

### 1. Short Answer Questions (2 & 3 Marks)

**Q1. Describe the structure of the Rural Local Government (Panchayati Raj System) in India.**  
* **Model Answer**:
  1. **Gram Panchayat**: Operating at the village level, it consists of elected ward members (*Panchs*) and a President (*Sarpanch*), supervised by the **Gram Sabha** (body of all adult voters).
  2. **Panchayat Samiti**: Formed at the block level by grouping several Gram Panchayats together, headed by a Block Development Officer / Samiti Chairperson.
  3. **Zilla Parishad**: The apex district-level body comprising all Panchayat Samitis, local MPs, MLAs, and district officials, headed by the **Zilla Parishad Chairperson**.

**Q2. How has the Language Policy of India strengthened national unity? Explain.**  
* **Model Answer**:
  * **No Single National Language**: Avoiding imposing Hindi on non-Hindi speaking states prevented linguistic conflict.
  * **22 Scheduled Languages**: Constitutional recognition of 22 languages ensured equality and regional dignity.
  * **Bilingual Official Use**: Allowing English alongside Hindi for official communication satisfied non-Hindi speaking regions (like Tamil Nadu) and consolidated national integration.

---

### 2. Original Predicted Board Question (2027 Examination Focus - 4 Marks)

**Q3. "Decentralisation post-1992 transformed local self-governance in India." Evaluate this statement by examining the 5 key provisions of the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments.**  
* **Model Answer**:
  * **Introduction**: Prior to 1992, local bodies were under direct state control, lacked financial independence, and suffered irregular elections. The 1992 Amendments provided constitutional status to local self-government.
  * **5 Key Constitutional Provisions**:
    1. **Mandatory Regular Elections**: State governments must conduct local elections every 5 years without arbitrary delays.
    2. **Reservation for SCs, STs, and OBCs**: Promotes inclusive social representation at the grass-root level.
    3. **33% Reservation for Women**: Empowered over 10 lakh women leaders in rural and urban local bodies.
    4. **State Election Commission (SEC)**: Created an independent constitutional authority in every state to conduct free and fair local elections.
    5. **State Finance Commission (SFC)**: Mandated state governments to share statutory revenue and administrative powers with local bodies.
  * **Conclusion**: These provisions transformed local self-government into the world's largest democratic experiment, deepening democracy at the grassroots level.
