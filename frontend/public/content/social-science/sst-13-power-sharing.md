## 2. Complete Syllabus-Aligned Revision Notes

### 2.1 Ethnic Composition of Belgium and Sri Lanka

#### A. Belgium: A Case of Diversity & Accommodation
* **Geographical Context**: A small European country, smaller in area than the state of Haryana in India, sharing borders with France, Netherlands, Germany, and Luxembourg. Population slightly over 1 crore.
* **Complex Ethnic Composition**:
  * **Flemish Region (North)**: **59%** of the country's population speaks **Dutch**.
  * **Wallonia Region (South)**: **40%** of the country's population speaks **French**.
  * **Remaining 1%**: Speaks **German**.
* **Capital City Brussels Exception**:
  * **80%** speak **French**, while **20%** speak **Dutch**.
  * *Tension Factor*: The French-speaking minority was relatively rich and powerful. This was resented by the Dutch-speaking community who got the benefit of economic development and education much later. This led to tensions between the two communities during the 1950s and 1960s, which was particularly acute in Brussels.

#### B. Sri Lanka: A Case of Diversity & Majoritarianism
* **Geographical Context**: An island nation off the southern coast of Tamil Nadu, India, with a population of about 2 crores.
* **Demographic Composition**:
  * **Sinhala Speakers**: **74%** of the population (mostly **Buddhist**).
  * **Tamil Speakers**: **18%** of the population, divided into two sub-groups:
    * *Sri Lankan Tamils* (**13%**): Native Tamils concentrated in the North and East.
    * *Indian Tamils* (**5%**): Descendants of plantation workers brought from India during the colonial period.
  * **Christians**: **7%** (both Tamil and Sinhala).
  * *Religions*: Most Sinhalas are Buddhist; most Tamils are Hindu or Muslim.

### 2.2 Majoritarianism in Sri Lanka and Its Consequences

* **Definition of Majoritarianism**: A belief that the majority community should be able to rule a country in whichever way it wants, by disregarding the wishes and needs of the minority.
* **Majoritarian Measures Adopted after Independence (1948)**:
  1. **Act of 1956**: Passed to recognize **Sinhala as the official language**, thus disregarding Tamil.
  2. **Preferential Policies**: Favoured Sinhala applicants for university positions and government jobs.
  3. **State Protection of Religion**: A new constitution stipulated that the state should protect and foster **Buddhism**.
* **Consequences and Impact on Tamils**:
  * Created a feeling of **alienation** among Sri Lankan Tamils.
  * Tamils felt that none of the major political parties led by Buddhist Sinhala leaders were sensitive to their language and culture.
  * Led to Tamil demand for **recognition of Tamil as an official language**, regional autonomy, and equality of opportunity in education and jobs.
  * By the 1980s, several political organisations were formed demanding an independent **Tamil Eelam** (state) in northern and eastern Sri Lanka.
  * Distrust transformed into a widespread **Civil War**, causing thousands of deaths, loss of livelihoods, and economic setback to the country.

### 2.3 Accommodation in Belgium (The Belgian Model)

Belgian leaders recognized regional differences and cultural diversities. Between 1970 and 1993, they **amended their constitution 4 times** to work out an arrangement that enabled everyone to live together within the same country.

#### Key Features of the Belgian Constitutional Model:
1. **Equal Representation in Central Government**: The Constitution prescribes that the number of Dutch and French-speaking ministers shall be equal in the Central Government. Special laws require the support of a majority of members from each linguistic group.
2. **Autonomy for State Governments**: Many powers of the Central Government were given to State Governments of the two regions. State Governments are not subordinate to the Central Government.
3. **Equal Representation in Brussels**: Brussels has a separate government in which both Dutch and French communities have equal representation (the French accepted equal representation in Brussels because the Dutch accepted equal representation in the Central Government).
4. **Community Government (*Samudayik Sarkar*)**: A unique third tier elected by people belonging to one language community—Dutch, French, and German-speaking—no matter where they live. This government has power regarding **cultural, educational, and language-related issues**.

### 2.4 Why Power Sharing is Desirable

Power sharing is desirable in democracy for two distinct sets of reasons:

```

    between social groups.                           SPIRIT of democracy.
 2. Ensures political stability.                  2. A democratic rule involves
 3. Prevents tyranny of majority                     sharing power with those
    which ruins majority too.                        affected by its exercise.
                                                  3. Legitimate govt is one where
                                                     citizens acquire a stake.
```

1. **Prudential Reasons (*Yukti-sangat Karon*)**:
   * Based on practical considerations and careful calculation of gains and losses.
   * Power sharing helps to **reduce the possibility of conflict** between social groups.
   * Social conflict often leads to violence and political instability; power sharing ensures the **stability of political order**.
   * Imposing the will of the majority may look attractive in the short run, but in the long run, it undermines the unity of the nation.

2. **Moral Reasons (*Naitik Karon*)**:
   * Based on the intrinsic worth and spirit of democracy.
   * Power sharing is the **very spirit of democracy**. A democratic rule involves sharing power with those affected by its exercise, and who have to live with its effects.
   * People have a right to be consulted on how they are to be governed.
   * A **legitimate government** is one where citizens, through participation, acquire a stake in the system.

### 2.5 Forms of Power Sharing in Modern Democracies

In modern democracies, power sharing takes four distinct structural forms:

| Form of Power Sharing | Mechanism / Description | Technical Name / Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Horizontal Distribution of Power** | Power is shared among different organs of government placed at the **same level** to exercise different powers. | **System of Checks and Balances**<br>*(Legislature, Executive, Judiciary)* |
| **2. Vertical Distribution of Power** | Power is shared among governments at **different levels**—a general central government for the whole country and regional/state governments. | **Federal Division of Power**<br>*(Union Govt $ightarrow$ State Govt $ightarrow$ Local Bodies)* |
| **3. Power Sharing among Social Groups** | Power is shared among different social, religious, and linguistic groups. | **Reserved Constituencies / Community Govt**<br>*(Belgium's Community Govt, India's SC/ST quotas)* |
| **4. Power Sharing among Political Parties & Pressure Groups** | Power is shared among political parties through competition and alliances, and influence exerted by pressure groups. | **Coalition Government**<br>*(Multi-party alliances, trade unions, farmers' lobbies)* |

## 3. Comparative Analytical Tables & Key Terminology

### 3.1 Comparison: Belgian Accommodation vs. Sri Lankan Majoritarianism

| Feature | Belgian Model (Accommodation) | Sri Lankan Model (Majoritarianism) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Approach to Diversity** | Recognized diversity and accommodated minority interests. | Disregarded minority rights and enforced majority dominance. |
| **Policy Enforcement** | Constitutional equality, equal ministerial power, Community Govt. | 1956 Sinhala Only Act, preferential jobs/university admissions. |
| **Political Outcome** | Avoided civic strife, prevented partition, became headquarters of European Union (EU). | Widespread alienation, violent clash, devastating Civil War. |
| **Core Principle** | Power Sharing (Prudential + Moral). | Majoritarian Hegemony. |

### 3.2 Key Technical Terms

* **Majoritarianism**: A belief that the majority community should be able to rule a country in whichever way it wants, disregarding minority needs.
* **Prudential**: Based on careful calculation of gains and losses; opposite to decisions based purely on moral considerations.
* **Civil War**: A violent conflict between opposing groups within a country that becomes so intense that it resembles a war.
* **Checks and Balances**: A system in horizontal power sharing where each organ of government checks the others, resulting in a balance of power.
* **Community Government**: A third tier of government in Belgium elected by people belonging to one language group, handling cultural, educational, and language issues.

## 5. Practice & Predicted Questions

### Practice Question 1 (Short Answer - 2 Marks)
**Question**: Why is horizontal distribution of power called a system of 'Checks and Balances'?  
**Model Answer**:  
Horizontal distribution shares power among organs at the same level—Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary. None of the organs can exercise unlimited power because each organ checks the other. For instance, judges are appointed by the Executive but can check the functioning of executive or laws made by legislature. This results in a balance of power among institutions.

---

### Practice Question 2 (Short Answer - 3 Marks)
**Question**: Explain how power is shared among political parties, pressure groups, and movements in a democracy.  
**Model Answer**:
1. **Political Parties**: Competition among political parties ensures that power does not remain in one hand. In the long run, power is shared among different political parties that represent different ideologies and social groups.
2. **Coalition Governments**: When two or more parties form an alliance to contest elections and win, they form a coalition government, directly sharing power.
3. **Pressure Groups & Movements**: Interest groups such as traders, farmers, workers, and industrialists participate in government committees or influence decision-making, ensuring indirect power sharing.

---

### Original Predicted Question for 2027 Board Exam (3 Marks)
**Question**: "Power sharing is not only good for democracy, but it is also the very spirit of democracy." Evaluate this statement highlighting Sri Lanka's failure and Belgium's success.  
**Model Answer**:
1. **Belgian Success**: Belgium recognized diversity and shared power across Dutch and French communities at central, state, and community levels. This prevented civic strife, maintained national unity, and made Brussels the EU headquarters.
2. **Sri Lankan Failure**: Sri Lanka adopted majoritarian policies favoring Sinhalas (1956 Act, job preferences), denying power to Tamils. This alienated Tamils, sparked demands for Tamil Eelam, and plunged the country into a devastating Civil War.
3. **Conclusion**: Sri Lanka proves that refusing to share power threatens national unity (Prudential), while Belgium proves that sharing power respects democratic rights and ensures stability (Moral).

**Q1. Which language is spoken by the majority population (59%) in Belgium?**  
(A) French  
(B) Dutch  
(C) German  
(D) Spanish  
*Answer*: **(B) Dutch**  
*Rationale*: 59% of Belgians live in the Flemish region and speak Dutch.

**Q2. In the capital city Brussels, what percentage of people speak French?**  
(A) 20%  
(B) 40%  
(C) 59%  
(D) 80%  
*Answer*: **(D) 80%**  
*Rationale*: Unlike the whole country where Dutch is majority, Brussels has 80% French speakers.

**Q3. Which major language policy act was passed in Sri Lanka in 1956?**  
(A) Declaring Tamil as official language  
(B) Declaring Sinhala as sole official language  
(C) Giving equal status to Sinhala and Tamil  
(D) Making English compulsory  
*Answer*: **(B) Declaring Sinhala as sole official language**  
*Rationale*: The 1956 Act disregarded Tamil and established Sinhala majoritarian dominance.

**Q4. What is the proportion of Sinhala speakers in Sri Lanka?**  
(A) 18%  
(B) 59%  
(C) 74%  
(D) 80%  
*Answer*: **(C) 74%**  
*Rationale*: Sinhalas form 74% of Sri Lanka's population.

**Q5. Between 1970 and 1993, how many times was the Belgian Constitution amended?**  
(A) 2 times  
(B) 3 times  
(C) 4 times  
(D) 5 times  
*Answer*: **(C) 4 times**  
*Rationale*: Belgian leaders amended the constitution 4 times to work out an accommodation model.

**Q6. What type of government in Belgium has power regarding cultural, educational, and language issues?**  
(A) Central Government  
(B) State Government  
(C) Community Government  
(D) Municipal Corporation  
*Answer*: **(C) Community Government**  
*Rationale*: Community government is elected by people belonging to one language community.

**Q7. Which organ is NOT part of the horizontal distribution of power?**  
(A) Legislature  
(B) Executive  
(C) Judiciary  
(D) State Government  
*Answer*: **(D) State Government**  
*Rationale*: State government is part of vertical (federal) distribution, not horizontal.

**Q8. The division of power between Central, State, and Local governments is called:**  
(A) Horizontal distribution  
(B) Vertical distribution  
(C) Community distribution  
(D) Coalition distribution  
*Answer*: **(B) Vertical distribution**  
*Rationale*: Vertical distribution involves sharing power among higher and lower levels of government.

**Q9. Prudential reasons for power sharing emphasize:**  
(A) Moral duty  
(B) Practical and better outcomes  
(C) Majority dominance  
(D) Cultural enforcement  
*Answer*: **(B) Practical and better outcomes**  
*Rationale*: Prudential reasons focus on reducing social conflict and ensuring stability.

**Q10. Which city was chosen as the headquarters of the European Union?**  
(A) Geneva  
(B) Brussels  
(C) Paris  
(D) Vienna  
*Answer*: **(B) Brussels**  
*Rationale*: Brussels was chosen due to Belgium's exemplary power-sharing model.

**Q11. Sri Lankan Tamils demanded an independent state called:**  
(A) Tamil Nadu  
(B) Tamil Eelam  
(C) Sinhala Eelam  
(D) Dravida Nadu  
*Answer*: **(B) Tamil Eelam**  
*Rationale*: By the 1980s, Tamil organisations demanded an independent Tamil Eelam in North/East Sri Lanka.

**Q12. What percentage of Sri Lanka's population is represented by Sri Lankan Tamils (natives)?**  
(A) 5%  
(B) 13%  
(C) 18%  
(D) 74%  
*Answer*: **(B) 13%**  
*Rationale*: Out of 18% total Tamils, 13% are native Sri Lankan Tamils and 5% are Indian Tamils.

**Q13. System of 'Checks and Balances' is associated with:**  
(A) Vertical division of power  
(B) Horizontal division of power  
(C) Power sharing among social groups  
(D) Majoritarianism  
*Answer*: **(B) Horizontal division of power**  
*Rationale*: Horizontal organs check each other, maintaining institutions' balance.

**Q14. In India, reserved constituencies for SCs and STs in parliament are an example of:**  
(A) Majoritarianism  
(B) Power sharing among social groups  
(C) Horizontal power sharing  
(D) Coalition government  
*Answer*: **(B) Power sharing among social groups**  
*Rationale*: Reserved seats give minority social groups representation in administration and legislature.

**Q15. Power shared among two or more political parties forming a government is called:**  
(A) Community Government  
(B) Federal Government  
(C) Coalition Government  
(D) Majoritarian Government  
*Answer*: **(C) Coalition Government**  
*Rationale*: A coalition government directly shares power among political party partners.
