### 1. Meaning and Core Components of a Political Party

#### Definition
A **political party** is a voluntary association of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government. They agree on some policies and programmes for the society with a view to promote the collective good.

#### Three Core Components
Every political party has three essential components:
1. **The Leaders**: Formulate policies, make key decisions, and guide party strategies.
2. **The Active Members**: Work at the grassroots level, run campaigns, organize rallies, and mobilize voters.
3. **The Followers**: Citizens and voters who sympathize with the party's ideology and vote for its candidates.

### 2. Seven Core Functions of Political Parties

Political parties perform fundamental functions in a representative democracy:

1. **Contesting Elections**: In most democracies, elections are fought mainly among the candidates put up by political parties. Parties select their candidates in different ways (e.g., top leaders choose candidates in India, whereas members choose candidates through primaries in the USA).
2. **Putting Forward Policies and Programmes**: Parties present different policies and programmes to the electorate. Voters choose from these options. In a democracy, a large number of similar opinions are grouped together to provide a direction in which policies can be formulated by the government.
3. **Making Laws for the Country**: Parties play a decisive role in making laws for a country. Formally, laws are debated and passed in the legislature, but most members belong to a political party and follow the party whip, regardless of their personal opinions.
4. **Forming and Running Governments**: Parties recruit leaders, train them, and make them ministers to run the government in the way they want. Executive heads (Prime Minister, Chief Ministers, Cabinet Ministers) are drawn from the ruling party.
5. **Playing the Role of Opposition**: Parties that lose in elections play the role of opposition to the parties in power. They voice different views, criticize the government for its failures or wrong policies, and mobilize opposition to the government.
6. **Shaping Public Opinion**: Parties raise and highlight key issues. They have lakhs of active members and activists spread all over the country. Many pressure groups are extensions of political parties. Parties launch movements for the resolution of problems faced by people.
7. **Providing Access to Government Machinery and Welfare Schemes**: For an ordinary citizen, it is much easier to approach a local party leader than a government officer. Parties serve as an essential link between citizens and the state administration.

### 3. Necessity of Political Parties

Why are political parties indispensable to modern representative democracies?

* **Limitation of Independent Candidates**: If there were no political parties, every candidate in an election would be independent. No one would be able to make promises to the electorate about major policy changes. The government might be formed, but its utility would remain uncertain.
* **Lack of Accountability for National Policy**: An elected representative would be accountable to their local constituency for what they do in the village or town, but no one would be responsible for how the country as a whole is run.
* **Requirement of Representative Democracy**: As societies become large and complex, they need an agency to gather different views on various issues and present these to the government. They need a mechanism to bring various representatives together so that a responsible government can be formed.

#### Coalition Government & Alliances in India
When several parties in a multi-party system join hands for the purpose of contesting elections and winning power, it is called an **alliance** or a **front**.
* In India, three major alliances emerged in the 2004, 2009, 2014, and 2019/2024 parliamentary elections:
  1. **National Democratic Alliance (NDA)** led by the BJP.
  2. **Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance (INDIA)** / Formerly United Progressive Alliance (UPA) led by the INC.
  3. **Left Front**.

### 5. National and State (Regional) Political Parties in India

In India, political parties are registered and recognized by the **Election Commission of India (ECI)**. The ECI offers special facilities to large and established parties, such as a unique **reserved election symbol** that only official candidates of that party can use. Parties that get this privilege and other special facilities are called **Recognised Political Parties**.

#### Key Recognised National Parties in India (Textbook Reference Profile)

1. **Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)**:
   * **Founded**: 1980 (re-formed from the erstwhile Bharatiya Jana Sangh, founded by Syama Prasad Mookerjee in 1951).
   * **Ideology**: Cultural nationalism (or *Hindutva*), integral humanism (Deendayal Upadhyaya), full territorial and political integration of Jammu & Kashmir, uniform civil code for all citizens, and ban on religious conversions.
   * **Alliance**: Leads the National Democratic Alliance (NDA).

2. **Indian National Congress (INC)**:
   * **Founded**: 1885 (one of the oldest parties in the world).
   * **Ideology**: Centrist party, secularism, welfare of weaker sections and minorities, economic reforms with a human face.
   * **Alliance**: Leads the Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance (INDIA) / erstwhile UPA.

3. **Communist Party of India - Marxist (CPI-M)**:
   * **Founded**: 1964 (split from CPI).
   * **Ideology**: Marxism-Leninism, supports socialism, secularism, and democracy; opposes imperialism and communalism; strong base in Kerala, West Bengal, and Tripura.

4. **Communist Party of India (CPI)**:
   * **Founded**: 1925.
   * **Ideology**: Marxism-Leninism, secularism, and democracy; opposed to forces of secessionism and communalism.

5. **Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)**:
   * **Founded**: 1984 under the leadership of Kanshi Ram.
   * **Ideology**: Represents and secures power for the *Bahujan Samaj* (Dalits, Adivasis, OBCs, and religious minorities); inspired by teachings of Sahu Maharaj, Mahatma Phule, Periyar Ramasami, and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar; strong base in Uttar Pradesh.

6. **National People's Party (NPP)**:
   * **Founded**: 2013 by P.A. Sangma.
   * **Ideology**: Focus on North-East India's regional identity, tribal rights, and development; first national party from North-Eastern India.

#### State / Regional Parties and Their Growing Importance
Parties other than national parties are classified by the ECI as 'State Parties' or regional parties. Examples include:
* **Aam Aadmi Party (AAP)** (Delhi, Punjab)
* **Samajwadi Party (SP)** & **Rashtriya Janata Dal (RJD)** (UP, Bihar)
* **Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK)** & **AIADMK** (Tamil Nadu)
* **All India Trinamool Congress (AITC)** (West Bengal)
* **Shiv Sena** (Maharashtra)

##### Role of Regional Parties in Indian Federalism
Over the last three decades, state parties have expanded their influence. No single national party was able to secure a majority on its own in the Lok Sabha between 1989 and 2014. As a result, national parties were compelled to form coalitions with state parties. This has made the Parliament of India politically more diverse and strengthened **federalism and democracy** in the country.

### 6. Four Major Challenges to Political Parties

Popular dissatisfaction and criticism have focused on four problem areas in the working of political parties:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     CHALLENGES TO POLITICAL PARTIES                     │
└────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────┬───────┴───────┬───────────────────┐
         ▼                   ▼               ▼                   ▼
┌─────────────────┐ ┌─────────────────┐ ┌─────────────────┐ ┌─────────────────┐
│1. LACK OF       │ │2. DYNASTIC      │ │3. MONEY & MUSCLE│ │4. LACK OF A     │
│   INTERNAL      │ │   SUCCESSION    │ │   POWER         │ │   MEANINGFUL    │
│   DEMOCRACY     │ │                 │ │                 │ │   CHOICE        │
└─────────────────┘ └─────────────────┘ └─────────────────┘ └─────────────────┘
```

1. **Lack of Internal Democracy within Parties**:
   * Concentration of power in one or a few top leaders.
   * Organizational elections are rarely held regularly.
   * Parties do not maintain membership registers or hold regular internal meetings.
   * Ordinary members do not get sufficient information on internal party matters and cannot influence decisions.

2. **Dynastic Succession**:
   * Top positions in many parties are controlled by members of one family.
   * Unfair to ordinary workers and harmful to democracy because people without adequate experience or popular support come to occupy positions of power.

3. **Growing Role of Money and Muscle Power**:
   * During elections, parties tend to nominate candidates who have or can raise lots of money.
   * Wealthy individuals and corporations who provide funds to parties influence party policies and decisions.
   * In some cases, parties support criminals who can win elections.

4. **Lack of a Meaningful Choice to Voters**:
   * In recent years, there has been a decline in the ideological differences among parties in most parts of the world.
   * For example, the difference between the Labour Party and the Conservative Party in the UK is very narrow.
   * In India as well, differences among major parties on economic policies have reduced. Voters cannot even elect different leaders because the same set of leaders keeps shifting from one party to another.

### 7. How Can Political Parties Be Reformed?

Reforming political parties requires both legal measures by the state and active participation by citizens.

#### A. Legal Measures Already Taken in India
1. **Anti-Defection Law**:
   * The Indian Constitution was amended (52nd Amendment, 1985 & 91st Amendment, 2003) to prevent elected MLAs and MPs from changing parties.
   * **Defection** means changing party allegiance from the party on whose ticket a person was elected to another party.
   * Now, if an elected MP or MLA changes parties, he/she loses his/her seat in the legislature. This has made defecting more difficult, though it has reduced internal dissent.
2. **Supreme Court Order on Affidavits**:
   * The Supreme Court passed an order to reduce the influence of money and criminals.
   * It is now mandatory for every candidate contesting elections to file an **affidavit** giving details of his/her property and criminal cases pending against him/her.
   * An **affidavit** is a signed document submitted to an officer where a person makes a sworn statement regarding their personal information.
3. **Election Commission Order on Organizational Elections**:
   * The Election Commission passed an order making it necessary for political parties to hold organizational elections and file their income tax returns.

#### B. Five Proposed Reform Suggestions
1. **Regulation of Internal Affairs**: A law should be made to regulate the internal affairs of political parties, making it compulsory to maintain a register of members, follow its own constitution, and hold open organizational elections.
2. **Mandatory Quota for Women**: It should be made mandatory for political parties to give a minimum number of tickets (about one-third / 33%) to women candidates.
3. **State Funding of Elections**: The government should give parties money to support their election expenses (in kind: petrol, paper, telephone, or in cash based on votes polled).
4. **Public Pressure and Campaigns**: Citizens, pressure groups, media, and movements can petition, publicize, and agitate to force political parties to reform.
5. **Direct Citizen Participation**: People who want reform must join political parties, as the quality of democracy depends on the degree of public participation.

### Question 5 (4 Marks - Essay / Long Answer with Internal Choice)
**Source**: BSER Secondary Board Examination 2024 Main Paper (Subject Code: 08)  
**Question**:  
Explain the major challenges faced by political parties in modern democracies.  
**OR**  
Suggest any four measures to reform political parties in India.

**Model Answer (Option 1 - Challenges)**:
Political parties in modern democracies face four major challenges:
1. **Lack of Internal Democracy**: Power is concentrated in the hands of one or a few top leaders. Organizational elections and membership registers are rarely maintained.
2. **Dynastic Succession**: Top party posts are often controlled by members of a single family, depriving ordinary workers of leadership opportunities based on merit.
3. **Role of Money and Muscle Power**: Parties tend to nominate wealthy candidates or those with criminal backgrounds who can fund election campaigns and win seats.
4. **Lack of Meaningful Choice**: Ideological differences among major parties have narrowed down, offering voters very little distinct policy choice.

**Model Answer (Option 2 - Reforms)**:
Four key measures to reform political parties in India include:
1. **Strict Implementation of Anti-Defection Law**: Prevent elected representatives from switching parties for personal gain.
2. **Mandatory Quota for Women**: Mandate at least one-third (33%) ticket reservation for women candidates in elections.
3. **State Funding of Elections**: The government should provide election funds to political parties (in cash or kind) to reduce the influence of black money.
4. **Mandatory Internal Democracy**: Pass a law compelling parties to hold regular organizational elections, maintain member registers, and abide by their constitution.

### Original Predicted Board Question 2027 (4 Marks - Long Answer)
**Question**:  
"Political parties are a necessary condition for a representative democracy." Evaluate this statement with four suitable arguments.

**Model Answer**:
The statement is completely valid. Political parties are indispensable to modern representative democracies for the following reasons:

1. **Alternative to Independent Chaos**: Without political parties, every candidate in an election would contest as an independent. No candidate could guarantee uniform national policies, leading to an unstable and fragmented government.
2. **Formulation of Collective Policies**: In large societies, millions of individual opinions exist. Political parties group similar views together to formulate coherent policies that direct government decision-making.
3. **Accountability for National Governance**: While independent candidates may look after their local constituency, political parties take collective responsibility for running the entire country.
4. **Linking Citizens to the State**: Parties provide ordinary citizens with easy access to government machinery, welfare schemes, and policy debates through local cadres.
