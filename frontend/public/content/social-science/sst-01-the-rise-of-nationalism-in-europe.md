## SECTION 3: Complete Syllabus-Aligned Revision Notes

### 3.1 The French Revolution and the Idea of the Nation (1789)

#### 1. First Clear Expression of Nationalism
Nationalism emerged clearly for the first time with the French Revolution in 1789. Before 1789, France was an absolute monarchy under a Bourbon king. The revolution transferred sovereignty from the absolute monarch to the body of French citizens.

#### 2. Measures Creating a Sense of Collective Identity (*la patrie* and *le citoyen*)
To build a shared national identity among French citizens, French revolutionaries introduced several key measures:
* **Fatherland and Citizen**: The ideas of *la patrie* (the fatherland) and *le citoyen* (the citizen) emphasized the notion of a united community enjoying equal rights under a single constitution.
* **National Symbols**: A new French flag, the tricolor, was chosen to replace the former royal standard.
* **Renaming Estates General**: The Estates General was elected by the body of active citizens and renamed the **National Assembly**.
* **Centrally Unified Administration**: A centralized administrative system was instituted, formulating uniform laws for all citizens within French territory.
* **Economic Integration**: Internal custom duties and dues were abolished, and a uniform system of weights and measures was adopted throughout France.
* **Linguistic Unity**: Regional dialects were discouraged, and French, as spoken and written in Paris, became the official national language.

#### 3. Napoleon's Civil Code of 1804 (Napoleonic Code)
Napoleon destroyed democracy in France, but in the administrative field, he incorporated revolutionary principles to render the system more rational and efficient:
* **Abolition of Birth Privileges**: The Civil Code of 1804 (Napoleonic Code) did away with all privileges based on birth.
* **Legal and Property Rights**: It established equality before the law and secured the right to property for French citizens.
* **Administrative Simplification**: Napoleon simplified administrative divisions across French-controlled territories (Dutch Republic, Switzerland, Italy, Germany).
* **Abolition of Feudalism**: Feudal systems were abolished, freeing peasants from serfdom and manorial dues.
* **Guild Restrictions and Transport**: Guild restrictions were removed in towns, and transport and communication systems were improved.

#### 4. Initial Enthusiastic Welcome vs. Later Hostility in Conquered Territories
* **Initial Welcome**: In Holland, Switzerland, and cities like Brussels, Mainz, Milan, and Warsaw, French armies were initially welcomed as "harbingers of liberty".
* **Turning to Hostility**: The initial enthusiasm turned into hostility when local populations realized that administrative reforms did not go hand-in-hand with political freedom. Increased taxation, censorship, and forced conscription into the French armies to conquer the rest of Europe outweighed the administrative benefits.

### 3.2 The Making of Nationalism in Europe

#### 1. Mid-18th Century European Context
In mid-18th-century Europe, there were no "nation-states" as we know them today. Germany, Italy, and Switzerland were divided into kingdoms, duchies, and cantons whose rulers had autonomous territories.
* **Habsburg Empire Case**: The Habsburg Empire ruling Austria-Hungary was a patchwork of many different regions and peoples. It included German-speaking Alpine regions (Tyrol, Austria, Sudetenland), Italian-speaking provinces (Lombardy, Venetia), Hungarian Magyars, and Polish aristocracy in Galicia. The only tie binding these diverse groups was a common allegiance to the emperor.

#### 2. The Aristocracy and the New Middle Class
* **Landed Aristocracy**: Socially and politically, a landed aristocracy was the dominant class on the European continent. They owned estates in the countryside and townhouses, spoke French for diplomacy, and were tied by dynastic marriage networks. However, numerically they were a small group.
* **The New Middle Class**: Industrial growth in Western and Central Europe led to the growth of towns and commercial classes. A new social group emerged: the working class, and a middle class composed of industrialists, merchants, and professionals. Among these educated, liberal middle classes, ideas of national unity and the abolition of aristocratic privileges gained popularity.

#### 3. Liberal Nationalism (What Liberalism Stood For)
The word 'liberalism' derives from the Latin root *liber*, meaning 'free'.
* **Political Sphere**:
  * Individual freedom and equality of all before the law.
  * Government based on consent and the end of autocracy and clerical privileges.
  * A constitution and representative government through parliament.
  * *Limitation*: Equality before the law did not necessarily stand for universal suffrage. In revolutionary France, property-owning men held vote rights; women and non-propertied men were excluded from political rights.
* **Economic Sphere**:
  * Freedom of markets and the abolition of state-imposed restrictions on the movement of goods and capital.
  * **Case Study — Zollverein (Customs Union of 1834)**: German-speaking regions in the early 19th century comprised 39 states created by Napoleon. Each state maintained its own currency, weights, and measures. A merchant traveling from Hamburg to Nuremberg had to pass through 11 custom barriers and pay a 5% duty at each. In 1834, a customs union (*Zollverein*) was formed at the initiative of Prussia and joined by most German states. It abolished tariff barriers, reduced currencies from over thirty to two, and created a national network of railways to stimulate economic mobility.

#### 4. A New Conservatism after 1815 and the Treaty of Vienna
* **Rise of Conservatism**: Following Napoleon's defeat at Waterloo in 1815 by allied European powers (Britain, Russia, Prussia, Austria), European governments were driven by conservatism. Conservatives believed that traditional institutions of state and society — monarchy, Church, social hierarchies, property, and family — should be preserved.
* **The Congress of Vienna (1815)**:
  * Representatives of Britain, Russia, Prussia, and Austria met at Vienna to draw up a settlement for Europe. The Congress was hosted by Austrian Chancellor **Duke Metternich**.
  * **Key Provisions of the Treaty of Vienna (1815)**:
    1. **Restoration of Dynasties**: The Bourbon dynasty, deposed during the French Revolution, was restored to power in France.
    2. **Containment of France**: France lost the territories Napoleon had annexed. A series of states were set up on France's borders to prevent French expansion (Kingdom of Netherlands including Belgium was set up in the north; Genoa was added to Piedmont in the south).
    3. **Territorial Rewards**: Prussia was given important new territories on its western frontiers; Austria received control of northern Italy; Russia was given part of Poland.
    4. **Preservation of German Confederation**: The German Confederation of 39 states set up by Napoleon was left untouched.
* **Repressive Nature**: Conservative regimes established in 1815 were autocratic. They did not tolerate criticism or dissent and imposed censorship laws to control newspapers, books, plays, and songs advocating liberty and freedom.

#### 5. The Revolutionaries and Giuseppe Mazzini
Because of autocratic repression, liberal-nationalists operated through secret revolutionary societies.
* **Giuseppe Mazzini (1807–1872)**:
  * Born in Genoa in 1807, Mazzini became an Italian revolutionary and member of the secret society of Carbonari.
  * Sent into exile in 1831 at age 24 for attempting a revolution in Liguria.
  * **Secret Societies**: Founded two underground societies — **Young Italy** (*Giovine Italia*) in Marseilles and **Young Europe** in Berne.
  * **Vision**: Mazzini believed God intended nations to be the natural units of mankind. Italy could not remain a patchwork of small states; it had to be forged into a single unified republic.
  * Metternich described Mazzini as *"the most dangerous enemy of our social order"*.

### 3.3 The Age of Revolutions: 1830–1848

#### 1. Revolutions of the 1830s
* **July Revolution in France (1830)**: Bourbon kings restored after 1815 were overthrown by liberal revolutionaries. A constitutional monarchy was installed under **Louis-Philippe**.
* **Metternich's Famous Quote**: Observing the French Revolution of July 1830, Austrian Chancellor Metternich famously remarked: *"When France sneezes, the rest of Europe catches cold."*
* **Belgian Uprising**: The July Revolution sparked an uprising in Brussels, leading to Belgium breaking away from the United Kingdom of the Netherlands.

#### 2. Greek War of Independence (1821–1832)
* Greece had been part of the Ottoman Empire since the 15th century.
* Growth of revolutionary nationalism in Europe sparked a struggle for independence among Greeks starting in 1821.
* Greeks received support from exiled compatriots and West Europeans who held respect for ancient Greek culture. Poets and artists lauded Greece as the "cradle of European civilization". English poet **Lord Byron** organized funds and went to fight, where he died of fever in 1824.
* **Treaty of Constantinople (1832)** officially recognized Greece as an independent nation.

#### 3. The Romantic Imagination and National Feeling
Nationalism developed not only through wars and territorial expansion, but also through culture. **Romanticism** was a cultural movement that sought to develop a particular form of nationalist sentiment.
* **Rejection of Reason and Science**: Romantic artists and poets criticized the glorification of reason and science, focusing instead on emotions, intuition, and mystical feelings.
* **Folk Culture (*Das Volk* & *Volksgeist*)**: German philosopher **Johann Gottfried Herder** (1744–1803) claimed that true German culture was to be discovered among the common people — *das Volk*. True spirit of the nation (*volksgeist*) was popularized through folk songs, folk poetry, and folk dances.
* **Case Study — Polish National Identity**:
  * At the end of the 18th century, Poland was partitioned by Great Powers (Russia, Prussia, Austria) and lost independence.
  * National feelings were kept alive through music and language. **Karol Kurpinski** celebrated the national struggle through operas and music, turning folk dances like the *polonaise* and *mazurka* into nationalist symbols.
  * Language was used as a weapon of national resistance against Russian rule. When Russian was enforced in schools, Catholic priests in Poland used Polish for religious instruction, leading to their arrest and exile to Siberia.

#### 4. Hunger, Hardship, and Popular Revolt (1830s–1840s)
* **Economic Distress in Europe**: The 1830s were years of severe economic hardship across Europe due to huge population growth, rural migration into overcrowded slums, and competition from cheap machine-made British imports.
* **Silesian Weavers' Revolt (1845)**: In 1845, weavers in Silesia led a revolt against contractors who supplied raw materials and drastically reduced payments. A crowd marched to the contractor's mansion demanding higher wages; eleven weavers were shot when the military intervened.
* **1848 Revolt in Paris**: In 1848, food shortages and widespread unemployment brought the population of Paris onto the streets. Louis-Philippe was forced to flee. A National Assembly proclaimed a French Republic, granted suffrage to all adult males above 21, and guaranteed the right to work through national workshops.

#### 5. 1848: The Revolution of the Liberals (Frankfurt Parliament)
Parallel to revolts of the poor, a revolution led by educated middle classes was underway.
* **Frankfurt Parliament (1848)**:
  * In German regions, political associations met at Frankfurt and decided to vote for an all-German National Assembly.
  * On **18 May 1848**, 831 elected representatives marched to take their seats in the **Church of St. Paul** in Frankfurt.
  * They drafted a constitution for a German nation to be headed by a monarchy subject to a parliament.
  * **Rejection by Friedrich Wilhelm IV**: When representatives offered the crown under these terms to Friedrich Wilhelm IV, King of Prussia, he rejected it and joined other monarchs to oppose the elected assembly.
  * **Failure and Disbandment**: The opposition of the aristocracy and military grew stronger. The assembly lost popular support because it was dominated by middle-class leaders who resisted the demands of workers and artisans. Troops were brought in and the parliament was forced to disband.
  * **Status of Women**: Women had actively participated in liberal movements, forming political associations and newspapers. Yet they were denied voting rights and admitted to the Frankfurt Parliament only as observers in the visitors' gallery.

### 3.4 The Making of Germany, Italy, and Britain

#### 1. Unification of Germany (1866–1871)
* **Prussian Leadership**: After the 1848 liberal failure, nationalist sentiments were mobilized by conservatives to achieve state power and national unity. Prussia took leadership of the German unification movement.
* **Otto von Bismarck**: Prussian Chief Minister **Otto von Bismarck** was the architect of German unification, operating through the Prussian army and bureaucracy ("Policy of Blood and Iron").
* **Three Wars over Seven Years**: Prussia fought three wars over seven years with **Denmark, Austria, and France**, ending in Prussian victories and completing unification.
* **Proclamation of Imperial Germany**: On **18 January 1871**, an assembly of German princes, military commanders, and Bismarck gathered in the unheated Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles to proclaim **Kaiser William I** of Prussia as German Emperor.
* **Modernization**: The new nation-state emphasized modernizing banking, currency, legal, and judicial systems across Germany.

#### 2. Unification of Italy (1859–1870)
* **Political Fragmentation**: In the mid-19th century, Italy was divided into seven states:
  * Northern Italy was under Austrian Habsburgs.
  * Center was ruled by the Pope.
  * Southern regions (Kingdom of Two Sicilies) were under Bourbon Kings of Spain.
  * Only **Sardinia-Piedmont** was ruled by an Italian princely house under King Victor Emmanuel II.
* **Key Architects of Italian Unification**:
  1. **Giuseppe Mazzini**: Provided ideological blueprint during the 1830s through Young Italy.
  2. **Count Cavour (Chief Minister)**: Led the diplomatic unification movement. Neither a revolutionary nor a democrat, Cavour engineered a tactful diplomatic alliance with France to defeat Austrian forces in 1859.
  3. **Giuseppe Garibaldi**: Led armed volunteers (Red Shirts) in south Italy. In 1860, Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand marched into the Kingdom of Two Sicilies and won the support of local peasants to drive out Spanish rulers.
* **Proclamation of United Italy**: In **1861**, **Victor Emmanuel II** was proclaimed King of United Italy. (Note: Much of the illiterate Italian peasantry remained unaware of liberal-nationalist ideology and thought *'La Talia'* was Victor Emmanuel's wife!).

#### 3. The Strange Case of Britain
Unlike France, Germany, or Italy, the formation of the nation-state in Britain was not the result of a sudden revolution, but of a long-drawn-out parliamentary process.
* **Ethnic Identities**: Primary identities in the British Isles were ethnic groups — English, Welsh, Scot, and Irish.
* **Growth of English Power**: As the English nation grew in wealth and importance, the English Parliament seized power from the monarchy in 1688.
* **Act of Union (1707)**: The Act of Union (1707) between England and Scotland resulted in the formation of the **United Kingdom of Great Britain**. England dominated Scotland, suppressing Scottish Gaelic language, Highland dress, and culture.
* **Incorporation of Ireland**: Ireland was deeply divided between Catholics and Protestants. The British supported Protestants to establish dominance over the Catholic majority. After a failed revolt led by **Wolfe Tone** and his United Irishmen (1798), Ireland was forcibly incorporated into the United Kingdom in **1801**.
* **British Symbols**: A dominant British national culture was promoted through the Union Jack (flag), "God Save Our Noble King" (national anthem), and the English language.

### 3.5 Visualising the Nation

#### 1. Personification and Allegories
Artists in the 18th and 19th centuries personified nations by portraying them as female figures (allegories). The female figure did not represent any real woman, but sought to give an abstract idea of the nation a concrete visual form.

#### 2. Marianne (Allegory of France)
In France, the national allegory was christened **Marianne**, a popular French name emphasizing the idea of a people's republic:
* **Attributes**: Drawn from Liberty and Republic — the Red Cap, the Tricolor, and the Cockade.
* **Public Visuals**: Statues of Marianne were erected in public squares to remind the public of national unity, and her figure was marked on coins and postage stamps.

#### 3. Germania (Allegory of Germany)
Germania became the visual allegory of the German nation:
* **Visual Attributes**:
  * **Oak Leaf Crown**: Germania wears a crown of oak leaves, as the German oak stands for heroism.
  * **Sword**: Readiness to fight.
  * **Olive Branch around Sword**: Willingness to make peace.
  * **Breastplate with Eagle**: Symbol of the German Empire and strength.
  * **Black-Red-Gold Tricolor**: Flag of liberal-revolutionaries of 1848.

### 3.6 Nationalism and Imperialism

#### 1. Transformation of Nationalism
By the last quarter of the 19th century, nationalism lost its idealistic liberal-democratic sentiment and turned into a narrow creed with limited ends. Nationalist groups became intolerant of each other and ready to go to war. Major European powers manipulated nationalist aspirations to further their imperialist aims.

#### 2. The Balkan Crisis (Primary Flashpoint)
The most serious source of nationalist tension in Europe after 1871 was the area called the **Balkans**.
* **Geographical Region**: Comprised modern-day Romania, Bulgaria, Albania, Greece, Macedonia, Croatia, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Slovenia, Serbia, and Montenegro, whose inhabitants were broadly known as the Slavs.
* **Ottoman Control & Explosive Conditions**: A large part of the Balkans was under the control of the Ottoman Empire. As the Ottoman Empire disintegrated and romantic nationalism spread, the region became explosive.
* **Rivalries among Balkan States**: Different Slavic nationalities struggled for independence. Balkan states were fiercely jealous of each other, each hoping to gain territory at the expense of others.
* **Big Power Rivalry**: The Balkans became the scene of intense rivalry among European powers — **Russia, Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Britain** — over trade, colonies, and naval supremacy. Each power sought to extend its control over the region, leading to a series of regional wars and ultimately triggering the **First World War in 1914**.

### Short Answer Questions (2–3 Marks)

#### Practice Question 1 [2 Marks]
**Question**: What was *Zollverein*? What was its economic significance in German unification?
* **Answer**: *Zollverein* was a customs union formed in 1834 at the initiative of Prussia and joined by most German states.
  * **Significance**: It abolished internal tariff barriers and reduced the number of unstandardized currencies from over thirty to two. By creating a unified economic space and expanding railway networks, it stimulated trade and harnessed economic nationalism to unify the German states.

#### Practice Question 2 [3 Marks]
**Question**: Describe the role of Giuseppe Garibaldi in the unification of Italy.
* **Answer**:
  1. Giuseppe Garibaldi was a celebrated Italian freedom fighter and leader of the armed volunteers known as the "Red Shirts".
  2. In 1860, Garibaldi led the famous "Expedition of the Thousand" into southern Italy and the Kingdom of Two Sicilies.
  3. He successfully won the support of local Italian peasants to drive out the Spanish Bourbon rulers, handing over conquered southern territories to King Victor Emmanuel II to unify Italy.

#### Practice Question 3 [3 Marks]
**Question**: Explain how Poland maintained its national identity after being partitioned by foreign powers.
* **Answer**:
  1. Even though Poland was partitioned at the end of the 18th century by Russia, Prussia, and Austria, Polish national feeling was kept alive through culture, music, and language.
  2. **Role of Music**: Composer Karol Kurpinski celebrated the national struggle through operas, transforming folk dances like the *polonaise* and *mazurka* into national symbols.
  3. **Role of Language**: Language served as a symbol of resistance against Russian domination. When Russian was imposed, Catholic priests persisted in using Polish for religious instruction, braving imprisonment and exile to Siberia.

### Long / Essay Answer Questions (3–4 Marks)

#### Practice Question 4 [4 Marks]
**Question**: "The 1830s were years of great economic hardship in Europe." Explain the statement with suitable arguments.
* **Answer**:
  1. **Enormous Population Expansion**: The first half of the 19th century witnessed an enormous increase in population across Europe, leading to widespread unemployment.
  2. **Rural-to-Urban Migration**: Job seekers from rural areas migrated to cities, living in overcrowded, unsanitary urban slums.
  3. **Industrial Competition**: Small producers in towns faced stiff competition from imports of cheap machine-made goods from England, where industrialization was far advanced.
  4. **Agricultural Distress & Feudal Dues**: In regions where aristocracy retained power, peasants struggled under feudal dues and obligations. Food shortages and bad harvests led to widespread pauperism in towns and country (e.g. 1848 Paris crisis).

#### Practice Question 5 [4 Marks]
**Question**: How did the Balkan region become a major source of nationalist tension in Europe leading to the First World War?
* **Answer**:
  1. **Geographical & Ethnic Diversity**: The Balkans was a region of geographic and ethnic variation (modern Romania, Bulgaria, Greece, Serbia, Bosnia, etc.) inhabited by Slavic peoples.
  2. **Disintegration of Ottoman Empire**: Most of the Balkans was ruled by the Ottoman Empire. The breakdown of Ottoman authority and the spread of romantic nationalism made the region highly explosive.
  3. **Intense Inter-Balkan Rivalry**: Each Slavic nationality claimed independence based on historical grounds. The Balkan states were fiercely jealous of one another, striving to expand territory at their neighbors' expense.
  4. **Big Power Imperialist Rivalry**: The region became an arena of intense rivalry among European Great Powers (Russia, Germany, Austria-Hungary, Britain) over naval supremacy and trade routes. This complex web of conflict led to a series of wars and directly caused the outbreak of World War I in 1914.

### Predicted Short Answer Question [3 Marks]
**Question**: Explain the dual role of language in European nationalism using examples from France and Poland.
* **Answer**:
  * **France (Language as Integration)**: French revolutionaries discouraged regional dialects and promoted French (as spoken in Paris) as the unified national language to foster collective national identity.
  * **Poland (Language as Anti-Colonial Resistance)**: Following Russian occupation, the Polish language was banned in schools. Polish clergy used Polish for Church services and religious instruction as a weapon of national resistance, preferring imprisonment over Russification.

### Predicted Long / Essay Question [4 Marks]
**Question**: Analyze how visual allegories were utilized by 19th-century European artists to personify nations. Contrast the symbols of Marianne and Germania.
* **Answer**:
  1. **Concept of Allegory**: Artists personified nations using female figures to transform abstract political ideas (liberty, republic, unity) into visual, concrete reality.
  2. **Marianne (France)**: Represents the popular French Republic. Key symbols include the Red Cap (liberty), Tricolor flag, and Cockade. Statues were erected in public squares to instill national unity.
  3. **Germania (Germany)**: Represents the German nation. Key symbols include the Crown of Oak Leaves (heroism), Sword (readiness to fight), Olive Branch (peace), and Breastplate with Eagle (imperial strength).

## SECTION 7: 15-Question Multiple Choice Question (MCQ) Quiz

#### Q1. When did the French Revolution take place, marking the first clear expression of nationalism?
(A) 1757
(B) 1789
(C) 1815
(D) 1848
* **Correct Answer**: **(B) 1789**
* **Explanation**: The French Revolution in 1789 led to the transfer of sovereignty from an absolute monarch to French citizens.

#### Q2. What was the Napoleonic Code of 1804 officially called?
(A) Treaty of Vienna
(B) Civil Code of 1804
(C) Edict of Nantes
(D) Constitution of 1791
* **Correct Answer**: **(B) Civil Code of 1804**
* **Explanation**: Napoleon introduced the Civil Code of 1804 to simplify administration, secure property rights, and abolish birth privileges.

#### Q3. Which Austrian Chancellor hosted the Congress of Vienna in 1815?
(A) Otto von Bismarck
(B) Duke Metternich
(C) Count Cavour
(D) Louis-Philippe
* **Correct Answer**: **(B) Duke Metternich**
* **Explanation**: Austrian Chancellor Duke Metternich hosted the Congress of Vienna after Napoleon's defeat.

#### Q4. Which secret society was founded by Giuseppe Mazzini in Marseilles?
(A) Carbonari
(B) Young Europe
(C) Young Italy
(D) Red Shirts
* **Correct Answer**: **(C) Young Italy**
* **Explanation**: Mazzini founded Young Italy (*Giovine Italia*) in Marseilles and Young Europe in Berne.

#### Q5. The Zollverein, formed in 1834, was a:
(A) Political Party
(B) Secret Society
(C) Customs Union
(D) Military Alliance
* **Correct Answer**: **(C) Customs Union**
* **Explanation**: *Zollverein* was a German customs union initiated by Prussia to remove tariff barriers.

#### Q6. Which treaty officially recognized Greece as an independent nation in 1832?
(A) Treaty of Vienna
(B) Treaty of Constantinople
(C) Treaty of Versailles
(D) Treaty of Paris
* **Correct Answer**: **(B) Treaty of Constantinople**
* **Explanation**: Signed in 1832, the Treaty of Constantinople recognized Greek independence from the Ottoman Empire.

#### Q7. Who said: "When France sneezes, the rest of Europe catches cold"?
(A) Napoleon Bonaparte
(B) Duke Metternich
(C) Kaiser William I
(D) Johann Gottfried Herder
* **Correct Answer**: **(B) Duke Metternich**
* **Explanation**: Metternich made this comment following the July 1830 revolution in France.

#### Q8. The German philosopher Johann Gottfried Herder claimed that true German culture resided in:
(A) The Aristocracy
(B) The Monarchs
(C) *Das Volk* (the common people)
(D) The Clergy
* **Correct Answer**: **(C) *Das Volk* (the common people)**
* **Explanation**: Herder asserted that folk culture (*das Volk*) expressed the true national spirit (*volksgeist*).

#### Q9. In which church was the Frankfurt Parliament convened on 18 May 1848?
(A) St. Peter's Basilica
(B) Church of St. Paul
(C) Westminster Abbey
(D) Notre-Dame Cathedral
* **Correct Answer**: **(B) Church of St. Paul**
* **Explanation**: 831 elected German representatives gathered in the Church of St. Paul in Frankfurt.

#### Q10. Who was the Chief Minister of Prussia and key architect of German unification?
(A) Count Cavour
(B) Giuseppe Garibaldi
(C) Otto von Bismarck
(D) Friedrich Wilhelm IV
* **Correct Answer**: **(C) Otto von Bismarck**
* **Explanation**: Bismarck carried out German unification through his Prussian army and "Blood and Iron" policy.

#### Q11. In January 1871, Kaiser William I was proclaimed German Emperor at:
(A) Berlin
(B) Frankfurt
(C) Palace of Versailles
(D) Vienna
* **Correct Answer**: **(C) Palace of Versailles**
* **Explanation**: The ceremony took place in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles in France.

#### Q12. Which Italian state was ruled by an Italian princely house prior to unification?
(A) Kingdom of Two Sicilies
(B) Lombardy
(C) Sardinia-Piedmont
(D) Venetia
* **Correct Answer**: **(C) Sardinia-Piedmont**
* **Explanation**: Only Sardinia-Piedmont was ruled by an Italian royal house under Victor Emmanuel II.

#### Q13. Who led the volunteer army known as the "Red Shirts" during Italian unification?
(A) Count Cavour
(B) Giuseppe Garibaldi
(C) Giuseppe Mazzini
(D) Victor Emmanuel II
* **Correct Answer**: **(B) Giuseppe Garibaldi**
* **Explanation**: Garibaldi led the Red Shirts to conquer the Kingdom of Two Sicilies in 1860.

#### Q14. What attribute on the visual figure of Germania represents heroism?
(A) Broken Chains
(B) Crown of Oak Leaves
(C) Olive Branch
(D) Sword
* **Correct Answer**: **(B) Crown of Oak Leaves**
* **Explanation**: In German symbolism, the oak stands for heroism.

#### Q15. Which turbulent European region became the primary flashpoint leading to World War I?
(A) The Baltic States
(B) The Iberian Peninsula
(C) The Balkans
(D) The Scandinavian Peninsula
* **Correct Answer**: **(C) The Balkans**
* **Explanation**: Slavic rivalries and Big Power competition in the Balkans triggered the outbreak of WWI in 1914.
