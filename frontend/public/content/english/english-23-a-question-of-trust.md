Chapter Metadata & Overview & OverviewMetadataChapter ID: english-23Title: A Question of TrustAuthor: Victor CanningGenre: Crime Mystery / Short Story / Dramatic IronyKey Setting: Shotover Grange (a wealthy country house)Key Characters:Horace Danby: A 50-year-old unmarried, respectable locksmith who secretly robs one safe a year to buy rare, expensive books.The Young Lady in Red: A clever, charming, confident woman thief who poses as the owner of Shotover Grange.Sherry: The pet dog at Shotover Grange.The Housekeeper: Horace's elderly housekeeper who worries about his health.OverviewA Question of Trust by Victor Canning is a humorous and ironic crime story that turns the phrase "honor among thieves" on its head7.The protagonist, Horace Danby, is a 50-year-old locksmith who is widely regarded as a good, respectable citizen16. However, he hides a secret double life: he suffers from a passion for rare, expensive books and plans one careful bank/safe robbery every year to fund his collection19.For his annual robbery, Horace targets Shotover Grange, meticulously studying its layout, wiring, and schedule for two weeks. When the servants leave for the movies, Horace breaks in. However, flowers on the table trigger his severe hay fever, causing him to sneeze repeatedly. Hearing the sneezes, a graceful young woman in a red dress enters, carrying the pet dog Sherry. She poses convincingly as the lady of the house, treating Horace with cool authority.When Horace begs for mercy, she promises to let him walk free if he opens the safe for her, claiming she forgot the combination and needs her jewels for a party that night. Naively trusting her, Horace takes off his gloves and cracking tools to open the safe. Two days later, Horace is arrested by the police—his fingerprints were found all over the room. The real owner of Shotover Grange turns out to be a 60-year-old gray-haired woman. Horace realizes he was outsmarted by a rival thief, ending up as an assistant librarian in prison, deeply angered whenever anyone mentions "honor among thieves"7.

### Section 1: Horace Danby’s Double LifePublic Identity: Horace Danby is 50 years old, unmarried, and lives with a housekeeper who worries about his health. He runs a successful lock-making business with two assistants and is viewed by society as good and respectable16.Secret Passion: Horace is an avid collector of rare, valuable books. Because he cannot afford them on his legitimate income, he robs one safe every year19. He plans each theft so meticulously that he steals just enough to last 12 months, purchasing his desired books through an agent19.Ethical Boundary: Horace is not a habitual, violent criminal; he robs only wealthy people, never uses weapons, and hates the idea of prison.Section 2: Planning the Shotover Grange RobberyThe Target: Horace decides to rob the safe at Shotover Grange, which contains jewels worth about 15,000 pounds. Selling them even for half price would buy the three rare books coming up for auction in autumn.Preparation: Horace spends two full weeks studying the house—its rooms, electric wiring, paths, and garden. He learns that the family is in London and the two servants are out at the movies.Entry: He cuts the burglar alarm wire, puts on a pair of rubber gloves, and enters through the kitchen door using a duplicate key.Section 3: The Robbery and Hay Fever AttackThe Flaw in Execution: Horace notices a large bowl of flowers sitting on the table in the drawing-room. Suffering from hay fever, the pollen causes a tickle in his nose, forcing him to sneeze loudly again and again.The Arrival of the Lady in Red: A quiet, confident voice speaks to him from the doorway: "What is it? A cold or hay fever?" A young, charming woman dressed in a bright red dress steps into the room, calming the barking pet dog, Sherry.The Impersonation: She acts completely at ease, speaking with the authority of the mistress of the house returning unexpectedly from London.Section 4: The Misplaced Trust and the TrapHorace's Plea: Panicked at the threat of prison, Horace begs her to let him go, promising never to steal again.The Bargain: The woman claims she needs her jewels that very night for a party, but forgot the numerical combination to her own safe. She promises to let Horace leave quietly if he opens the safe for her.The Fatal Mistake: Eager to escape arrest, Horace agrees. In his rush and excitement, he takes off his rubber gloves to handle his fine tools and open the safe without leaving glove marks on the dial. He hands her the jewels and leaves happily, believing he has secured his freedom.Section 5: The Arrest and Final IronyThe Investigation: Two days later, a policeman arrests Horace for the jewel robbery at Shotover Grange.The Evidence: Horace's fingerprints were found all over the drawing-room, table, and safe because he had opened it bare-handed!The Revelation: When Horace tries to explain that he opened the safe for the owner's wife, the real mistress of the house—a 60-year-old, sharp-tongued woman with gray hair—denies his story completely.Outcome: Horace is sentenced to prison, where he becomes the assistant librarian. He frequently thinks about the clever young lady in red who tricked him, getting furious whenever anyone speaks of "honor among thieves"7.


## 4. Themes & Character Analysis & Key ConceptsCore ThemesDramatic Irony & The Cheat Cheated: Horace believes he is the smartest thief in the room, yet he falls victim to a far more cunning, theatrical imposter7.Deceptive Appearances: Just as Horace's respectable locksmith facade hides a thief, the young woman's charming, elegant demeanor hides a ruthless criminal16.Misplaced Trust & "Honor Among Thieves": Horace naively trusts a fellow criminal based on a verbal promise, proving that there is no guaranteed honor in the world of crime7.Character Analysis1. Horace DanbyTraits: Meticulous, polite, non-violent, educated, naive, book-loving19.Duality: A "respectable" citizen who makes locks by day, but breaks safes once a year to fuel his intellectual addiction to rare literature16. His fatal flaw is his gullibility when under pressure.2. The Young Lady in RedTraits: Clever, calm, manipulative, highly observant, theatrical, fearless.Role: The ultimate antagonist who uses psychology, charm, and Horace's hay fever against him. She tricks Horace into doing the physical work and leaving incriminating fingerprints while she walks away with 15,000 pounds in jewels.

**Model Answer:** The young lady in red entered the drawing-room with extreme confidence and composure, holding the pet dog Sherry. She spoke with the natural authority of the house owner. When Horace pleaded to be spared from prison, she offered to let him go if he opened the safe, claiming she forgot the combination. Naively trusting her, Horace removed his gloves, cracked the safe, and handed her the jewels, leaving his fingerprints for the police.How does "A Question of Trust" demonstrate the concept of dramatic irony?
**Model Answer:** The story uses dramatic irony by presenting a thief who believes he has planned the perfect robbery, only to be outwitted by a rival thief. Horace Danby takes great pride in his meticulous preparation and non-violent ethics. Yet, he fails to recognize that the charming woman in red is an imposter. The ultimate irony is that Horace ends up in prison for a robbery where he walked away empty-handed.
